# What the worked examples forced into the language

Design archaeology. Companion to [`scope-and-ambition.md`](scope-and-ambition.md) (forward-looking doctrine) and [`runtime-semantics.md`](runtime-semantics.md) (what the kernel means). This file records each design move and the worked example that forced it.

The entries are compressed - each a Forced-by/Landed stub, with the per-PR detail and the considered-and-rejected alternatives left to git, and the rationale that became doctrine referenced inline. The one fuller passage is the parser-arc narrative below, kept whole because it orients a reader of the parser rather than recording a single move.

## Pre-parser-arc IR moves (compressed)

These entries record kernel work in the order it happened. Each says what was forced and what landed.

### `Value::Subject` IR variant

**Forced by:** claim standing example. Purposes like `bank_debt_service` needed to appear as constant references in invariant bodies; pre-existing IR had only `Value::Decimal`.

**Landed:** `Value::Subject(String)` variant; matched in `unify_args` and resolved by `resolve_term`. Constant of any other kind earns its variant when a future example needs one.

### The `require`-vs-`invariant` semantic distinction

**Forced by:** claim standing example. A rule like "every X claim implies an active Y" written as an invariant would either reject revocations or cascade-retract history. Neither matches the real semantic (decisions made under valid standing remain valid even after revocation).

**Resolution:** `require` is the admission gate (checked at admission, never re-checked); `invariant` is the eternal rule (must always hold against admitted state). The two answer different questions and are not interchangeable. This is the most reused doctrine in the codebase; pinned in [`runtime-semantics.md`](runtime-semantics.md).

### Currentness and standing as distinct constructs

**Forced by:** the verified-revenue and claim-standing examples together. Currentness ("which claim is in force now?") and standing ("which claim may be relied on for this purpose?") share the same lower-level mechanic - a separate retractable claim that confers a property on another, append-only claim - but they index differently. Collapsing them loses the ability to say "the same verification is in force for the bank but not for investor reporting."

**Pattern:** when modelling "which X is canonical for purpose Y?", first decide whether currentness alone suffices or whether purpose-specific standing is needed.

### History-as-append-only

**Forced by:** the verified-revenue and claim-standing examples. Discipline: content claims are append-only; pointer / admissibility claims are retractable; lineage / revocation claims are append-only again. The middle bucket is the only retractable kind.

### Generic standing on any subject

**Forced by:** claim standing example. `grant_standing` will admit `AdmissibleFor(any_subject, any_purpose)` even if the subject names no claim. The decision transformation catches the absence via its own `require`. Tightening this requires typed predicate declarations (an affordance still awaiting a worked example).

### Derived claims and `Expr::Sub`

**Forced by:** trial balance over the double-entry ledger. "What is the balance of every account?" had no expression in the runtime; computing it in plain Rust put the logic outside the governed model.

**Landed:** `DerivedClaim { predicate, keys, values, domain }` with `DerivedValue { name, expr }` per computed value. `Expr::Sub(Box<Expr>, Box<Expr>)` for decimal subtraction. `enumerate_derived` reuses `find_matches` for binding enumeration. v0 derived claims are not visible to invariants or transformations, not in `State.claims`, not persisted, not recursive, not as-of - each deferred deliberately. (Most of those deferred concerns landed later via the read-side surfacing PR.)

### As-of evaluation

**Forced by:** the read-side surfacing of derived claims revealed that auditors need historical state, not just current. Without an as-of facility, the only escape is bespoke SQL outside Morpholog.

**Landed:** no kernel change - as-of is a question of which `State` you hand to the kernel. `reconstruct_state_at(pool, transition_id)` on `morpholog-postgres` replays the audit log in `(committed_at, transition_id)` order. `list_claims_at` and `list_derived_at` are thin wrappers. `PgError::TransitionNotFound` is the unknown-id failure mode.

### `ReplaySet` (audit-log replay working set)

**Forced by:** the as-of benchmark surfaced a quadratic in `reconstruct_inner` at 10K-100K audit rows. The pattern is the third instance of the same fix: replacing a `Vec` + linear-scan membership check with a HashMap-backed structure that gives amortised O(1) assert / retract.

**Landed:** `ReplaySet` inside `morpholog-postgres`, used by `reconstruct_inner`. Replay at 100K rows became feasible (~1.5s, was projected ~8 minutes).

### `Transition` value object and `audit.actor`

**Forced ahead of an example** (the only ahead-of-example deviation in the codebase). The next authority example would need both the plumbing AND the consultation primitive in one PR; splitting them keeps each honest.

**Landed:** `Transition { transformation_name, args, actor }` in `morpholog-core`. `propose()` takes a `Transition` separately from the `Transformation`. `audit.actor` column on PostgreSQL. Required `--actor` flag on `morpholog propose`. `system_actor()` helper for runtime-initiated transitions (placeholder until lineage forces a richer model). `Term::Actor` deliberately did NOT land here - that's the consultation primitive, forced by the next example.

### `Term::Actor` consultation primitive

**Forced by:** approval-controls example. PR #35 plumbed the actor through the audit log; nothing in the IR could yet consult it. `require MayApprove(actor, doc_type)` needs an IR construct for "the proposing actor."

**Landed:** `Term::Actor` variant on the existing `Term` enum, no payload. Resolves to the actor of the proposed transition; raises `EvalError::UnboundActor` if no transition is in scope. Threaded through every binding-matching function. Authority checks belong in `require`, not invariants - an invariant body that reaches for `Term::Actor` errors at evaluation. Considered (and rejected): stashing the actor under a reserved binding key in `Bindings`.

### `Expr::Le` decimal comparison primitive

**Forced by:** approval-limits example. Unconditional authority ("may approve") gave way to quantitative ("may approve up to N"); `amount <= limit` had no IR expression.

**Landed:** `Expr::Le(Box<Expr>, Box<Expr>)` for decimal comparison. Both operands must evaluate to `EvalValue::Decimal` (anything else surfaces as `TypeMismatch`). Inclusive semantics: `amount = limit` admits. `Lt`, `Gt`, `Ge` each earn their place when an example forces them - the "complete set" temptation was deflected.

### `Expr::Add` decimal addition primitive

**Forced by:** insurance-claim-settlement example. Cumulative limit ("sum of payments + this payment <= cap") had no IR expression.

**Landed:** `Expr::Add(Box<Expr>, Box<Expr>)` for decimal addition. Decimal-only with `TypeMismatch` for other kinds, matching `Sub` / `Le`. Operands compose with `Sum` for cumulative checks.

### `Value::Date`, `EvalValue::Date`, `Expr::DateLe`

**Forced by:** clinical-trial-enrolment example. Inclusive `[from, to]` civil-date windows on a randomisation date had no expression. Comparing dates via `<=` (decimal) would fail at type-check; comparing them as subject literals would degrade to lexicographic ordering (wrong as soon as a year changes digits).

**Landed:** `Value::Date(String)` IR literal in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. `EvalValue::Date` runtime value. `Expr::DateLe` separate from `Expr::Le` - the kernel keeps them as distinct primitives so each type-checks its operands. Doctrine: no operator overloading by operand type. Strict-ordering date comparators (`before`, `after`, `on_or_after`), date arithmetic, intervals, business calendars, and time-of-day are deferred until an example forces them.

### `Stmt::BindOne` (refactor)

**Forced by:** a refactor arc, not a worked example. The "require to gate, then `let` and `value_of` to extract" workaround was idiomatic but indirect: three lines for one canonical move ("match this unique claim and extend the bindings").

**Landed:** `Stmt::BindOne(Expr)` as a first-class statement. Semantics: evaluate a predicate-shaped expression, require exactly one match, treat the match's bindings as the next context. The old workaround still works; new examples use the primitive.

### `PredicateDecl` + `Program.predicates`

**Forced by:** the refactor arc establishing predicate declarations as the canonical vocabulary of a programme. Pre-existing programmes were "vocabulary by implicit usage"; the runtime had no way to validate arity, kinds, or document the predicate surface.

**Landed:** `PredicateDecl { name, args: Vec<PredicateArgDecl> }` on `Program`. Strict arity validation: a claim with the wrong number of args for its predicate is `EvalError::ArityMismatch`. Kind information is documentation today; future static checks may consult it. `predicate(...)` DSL builder helps construct decls fluently in the Rust IR.

### `propose_with_trace` (kernel) + `propose_against_pg_with_trace` (PG adapter)

**Forced by:** the refactor arc preparing for legibility tooling. The runtime needs to explain *why* a proposal succeeded or rejected, not just whether. Without trace, the only diagnostic available to a rejected proposer was "rejected" + the error variant.

**Landed:** `propose_with_trace` returns `TracedProposal::{Completed, Errored}` - trace flows on both success and kernel-error paths. The PG-adapter equivalent `propose_against_pg_with_trace` returns `Result<PgTracedOutcome, PgError>` with `PgTracedOutcome::{Outcome, KernelErrored}` carrying trace through the kernel-error path; PG-layer errors flow through `Err(PgError)` and drop trace. CLI `--trace` emits structured JSON.

### Predicate-scoped loading

**Forced by:** the `--noise-claims` benchmark scenario. Loading the full claim set on every proposal scales linearly with admitted-state size; programmes that touch a small fraction of predicates were paying for the whole table.

**Landed:** `propose_against_pg` loads only the predicates the transformation and its invariants reference. Read and write paths are separately scoped via `predicates_referenced_by_*` walkers. Bench surfaced the win at 100K-row scale.

### Expression failure-walk on rejection paths

**Forced by:** the same legibility arc. A rejected `require` says "rejected" but not which sub-expression. For a long boolean chain, that's the wrong granularity; the auditor needs the specific clause that didn't hold.

**Landed:** `find_failing_subexpr` walks the rejected expression and identifies the smallest sub-expression responsible. Trace exposes it via `TraceEntry::RequireRejected.failing_sub_expression`. `find_conjunction` and the failure-walker share the binding-flow rule: each conjunct sees the prior conjunct's extensions; a walker that re-evaluates each conjunct against the original bindings is wrong.

## What deliberately stayed minimal across the early examples

Each was considered during a specific example and deferred. They live here so the deferred reasoning survives.

| Considered | Why deferred |
|---|---|
| `ReliedOnBy(decision_id, claim_id)` decision-snapshot pattern | The simpler `require`-based design proved sufficient. |
| `RevocationLifted` claim or grant-supersession for re-grant | Revocation is terminal in v0. Re-granting needs a lifecycle model an example doesn't yet force. |
| Temporal qualification claims (`OccurredOn`, `EffectiveFor`, `KnownAsOf`) | Candidates for the as-of operator; no example yet needs them. |
| Strict comparison (`<`, `>`, `>=`) | `Le` and `DateLe` are the only comparators today. Strict variants land when an example needs strictness. |
| Predicate-pattern matching (one authority claim governs a predicate family) | Higher-order authority shape; awaits an example. |
| Cumulative or time-bounded limits ("up to N per day") | Needs `Sum` over a time-bounded subset of admitted state. |
| Cascading retraction of historical decisions on standing revocation | Contradicts "history is preserved." |
| Per-example PostgreSQL schemas | All examples share the canonical runtime schema; predicates differ in what they admit, not in storage shape. |

## Parser arc (closed)

The `.morph` surface was built incrementally across eight PRs (P1 through P3c): predicate declarations, then expression syntax in isolation, then the bounded forms, then programme-level integration (invariants, then transformations + statements, then derived claims), then civil-date comparison, then the round-trip property test that closed it. The per-PR blow-by-blow lives in git; what follows is the load-bearing set of decisions a reader of the parser needs.

**The premise.** Programmes were Rust IR via the `dsl` module; the natural reader of a Morpholog programme is a domain expert, not a Rust developer. The arc gives the IR a surface. The crate is `morpholog-surface`, not `morpholog-parser`, because it also hosts the formatter and will host source-mapping and LSP tooling - one crate per source-aware concern, not one per tool.

**Pipeline.** Character lexer (`chumsky`) -> layout pass (virtual `Indent`/`Dedent`) -> structural parser -> `morpholog_core::Program`. Diagnostics (`ariadne`) carry byte-offset spans and render to line/column/caret; spans live entirely in `morpholog-surface` and never leak into kernel errors, which are runtime-shaped. Recovery collects multiple diagnostics per run by syncing at the next top-level keyword. Top-level declarations interleave freely (predicate / intent / invariant / transformation / derived in any order); duplicate-name detection runs parser-side so diagnostics carry both spans.

**Position A, made enforceable.** The surface is more readable than the IR but never more powerful (the doctrine in [`scope-and-ambition.md`](scope-and-ambition.md)). Every place the surface could outrun the IR, the parser refuses:

- `In` and claim-call arguments are `Term`-only in the IR, so `a + 1 in xs` and `Foo(x + 1, y)` are parse errors, not silently ill-shaped IR. (`Neq` was `Term`-only here too until the comparator-collapse PR lifted it to expressions; see below.)
- No `true`/`false` literals: the IR has no `Value::Bool` to lower them to. Reserved at the lexer, rejected at the parser.
- `value Pred(args) [default expr]` is claim-pattern-shaped, not a general `value(target | body)` query - the latter would be more expressive than `Expr::ValueOf` can represent. (A doctrine-table row had the wrong shape; corrected before any parser code committed it.)
- Transformation parameters carry no kinds, because `Transformation.parameters: Vec<String>` doesn't.

**Layout.** Blocks are indentation, not braces. Spaces only - a tab in indentation is a diagnostic, refusing the tabs-vs-spaces ambiguity rather than guessing. Parens disable layout, so a long expression spans lines freely inside them. No virtual `Newline` token: every statement and declaration starts with its own keyword, which is the boundary. Nested blocks (a `for` body inside a transformation) use a `recursive` parser bounded by matched `Indent`/`Dedent` pairs; each `: <body>` consumer (invariant, `exists`, `forall`) accepts inline or indented form via a local `(Indent body Dedent | body)` choice.

**Literals and quantifiers.** `@YYYY-MM-DD` for civil dates, `#NAME` for subjects - sigils resolve the arithmetic ambiguity (`2026 - 05 - 22`) with no lexer complexity; decimals are carried as strings to preserve exactness. `forall x in <source>` auto-lifts a bare-variable source to `Expr::In(Var(x), source)` so the kernel can iterate, while a claim-shaped source passes through. `in` is positionally disambiguated: structural inside `forall`/`for`, a membership comparator elsewhere. Quantifier bodies are greedy to the end of the enclosing expression; composition with outer terms needs parens. `sum`'s target is restricted to a variable name.

**`on_or_before` for date comparison.** Civil-date `<=` lowers to `Expr::DateLe`, a separate kernel primitive from decimal `Le`. Rather than dispatch `<=` on operand kind (the parser has no type environment, and the `DateLe` design deliberately separated the two), the surface uses a keyword that reads as a regulatory clause: `effective_from on_or_before randomisation_date`.

**Round-trip property test.** `tests/round_trip.rs` runs every `all_programs()` entry through `format_program -> parse_program -> assert_eq`, catching formatter drift and parser regressions together; a new worked example extends coverage for free. Building it forced the formatter to emit canonical *parseable* text (infix booleans, `admit`/`bind` verbs, `#`/`@` sigils, `on_or_before`) instead of debug-style output, and surfaced the actor-shadowing fix: transformation parameters named `actor` collided with the auto-mapped `Term::Actor`, so they were renamed `principal` - clearer anyway, since the parameter is the *subject* of an authority claim, not the proposer.

The arc is complete for v0: every worked example parses end-to-end. `morpholog run <file.morph>` (proposing a user programme against PostgreSQL) and the enriched `morpholog check` build on the closed surface.

## After the parser arc

Post-parser kernel and tooling moves, through the legibility arc, each distilled to a Forced-by/Landed stub once it settled (the per-PR detail and the considered-and-rejected alternatives live in git).

### `Expr::Or` predicate-shaped disjunction

**Forced by:** chess `SingleCapturePerMove` (`after = before or after = before - 1`); landed a PR ahead of chess to keep that PR focused on `Pre`. Independent rationale: every other predicate-shaped composer was already first-class, and De Morgan's `not (not A and not B)` punishes the natural surface form.

**Landed:** `Expr::Or(Vec<Expr>)` mirroring `And`'s flattened shape; `find_disjunction` concatenates branch bindings (no dedup, matching `find_conjunction`); surface `or` at standard precedence. `find_failing_subexpr` returns `None` for `Or` - when a disjunction fails every branch failed, so blaming one would mislead.


### `Expr::Pre` transition-invariant primitive

**Forced by:** the chess transition-invariants example (after Murat Demirbas's [Chess invariants](https://muratbuffalo.blogspot.com/2026/05/chess-invariants.html) post). State invariants (over one state) and transition invariants (over a pre/post pair) are distinct kinds; "MoveCount advanced by one" needs both states. Chess was chosen over a business retrofit because no existing example had admitted operational state to compare across the transition.

**Landed:** `Expr::Pre(Box<Expr>)` - a wrapper opting a subtree into pre-state evaluation; post stays the default, pre is opt-in (the inverse of TLA+ priming, because Morpholog has one state at a time by design). Zero migration, composes with `Sum`/`ValueOf`/quantifiers, and preserves the non-commutativity of `pre(forall ...)` vs `forall ... pre(...)`. `EvalError::PreStateUnavailable` when `Pre` is reached with no pre-state in scope; phrased about evaluation context, not AST position, so future both-state contexts share it. The deferred `EvalContext` refactor was rejected here on subtraction grounds and landed one PR later, once a second contextual arg forced it.


### Insurance retrofit: `PolicyHeadroom` conservation via `pre(...)`

**Forced by:** closing the `Expr::Pre` PR's deferred business case with a real conservation rule. Insurance was chosen over the ledger because it already lives around aggregate-cap discipline and the ledger retrofit would need a wider remodel.

**Landed:** admitted `PolicyHeadroom(policy_id, remaining)` with `at_most_one_headroom_per_policy` (uniqueness) and `paid_implies_headroom` (existence, so the conservation guard is not vacuous); `authorise_settlement` reads headroom via `bind_one`, retracts it, and asserts the decremented one. Transition invariant `headroom_consumed_by_payment` uses the sum-of-new-payments delta form - `after = before - sum(amt | SettlementPaid(...) and not pre(SettlementPaid(...)))` - not a per-row equation: the per-row draft passed each row while a multi-payment transition with two same-amount settlements consumed headroom twice (caught in review). Bundled the `EvalContext { state, pre_state, bindings, actor }` evaluator refactor, now honestly forced by `Pre`'s second contextual arg.


### Enriched `morpholog check`: kind/type compatibility

**Forced by:** the input/output boundary made authoring trustworthiness the bottleneck. Decimal-in-subject-slot, `<=` against a date, arithmetic on a subject literal all only surfaced at runtime as `EvalError::TypeMismatch`.

**Landed:** `morpholog_core::kindcheck`, merged with the structural pass into one `Vec<ValidationError>`. Kind inference refines on observation (`Any` stays unconstrained until a concrete use - the "`Any` everywhere" alternative was rejected for losing the most valuable refinement pattern). The walker mirrors the runtime quartet (`Require` against a cloned env, `BindOne`/`Let` against the live one) and shadows `Sum`/`Forall`/`Exists` binders via `KindEnv::with_shadow` so a loop-local cannot collide with an outer variable, without which it would reject correct shadowing or pass programmes the runtime rejects.


### `IntentDecl`: outbox vocabulary as a declared first-class kind

**Forced by:** the KYC example, where a mistyped `emit SARFiled(...)` would silently route to nowhere - no analyst sees it, and the regulatory breach is invisible until a downstream auditor notices the gap. Predicates had `PredicateDecl`; intents were still stringly-typed and kindcheck skipped `Stmt::Emit`.

**Landed:** `IntentDecl` mirroring `PredicateDecl`; `Program.intents`; surface `intent X(args)`; strict from day one (every `emit` targets a declared intent). The foundational move was the `VocabularyKind { Predicate, Intent }` enum: the two vocabularies share every diagnostic shape, so the predicate-specific error variants were generalised (`UndeclaredPredicate` -> `Undeclared { vocabulary, name, context }`) rather than duplicated, and `PredicateArgDecl` became `ArgDecl`. A third declared vocabulary would slot in without further renaming.


### Static-analysis pass: one visitor, binding flow, actor context, and a depth floor

**Forced by:** the kind check had landed as a second walker beside the structural pass, and the next checks (unbound variables, value/predicate shape, actor context) all needed the same traversal and scope notion. A second binding-aware check made the two-walker shape stop paying - separate walks would each re-derive scope and drift from the runtime's binding rules.

**Landed:** `kindcheck` became `check`; `validate_program` is now a duplicate-declaration pass plus one `check_program` traversal carrying a `Scope { kinds, bound }`, cloned at the quartet's boundaries (`require`, `sum`, `for`, `or`-branches) so the non-export rules fall out of structure. Unbound-variable detection follows the evaluator exactly, where the subtlety lived: a disjunction exports only the *intersection* of names its branches bind (the KYC `(clean or adjudicated_clear) and ... expires` invariant relies on it), and `in` is a generator not a use - both caught by running the check over every worked example as the regression gate. Added `ExpectedPredicateExpression`, actor-in-wrong-context (`ActorNotAvailable`), a nesting-depth floor (`NestingTooDeep` - the enforceable form of "validate untrusted IR before proposing it", since `propose` trusts the IR it is handed), and `PgError::DuplicateIntent` to separate a modelling bug from a transient DB error.


### Counting via a constant `sum` target (and dropping `Sum.binding`)

**Forced by:** the chess example wanted to count pieces, and nothing in the kernel could express it. The doctrine had already flagged relaxing `sum`'s variable-only target when an example forced it.

**Landed:** the `sum` target now accepts a decimal literal, so `sum(1 | body)` counts bindings - no evaluator change (the target was already resolved per match), only a parser relaxation and a formatter that renders the target from the term. Chess gained `piece_count_matches_board`, exact-one-king per colour (count `= 1`, which forbids capturing a king), and a pawn bound. The same change retired the vestigial `Expr::Sum.binding` field, which duplicated the target name and was ignored by the evaluator. A dedicated `Expr::Count` was rejected as redundant with `sum(1 | body)`.


### The full comparator set per kind

**Forced by:** legibility, not a worked example - the deliberate exception. The strict forms are derivable (`a > b` is `not(a <= b)`), but the kernel has no `>` to render, so the formatter would print `amount > limit` back as `not (amount <= limit)` everywhere it shows a programme. Derivability buys nothing for legibility, which for the auditor reading formatted output is a core value - the third principle's "the easy case is verbose, so the shape is wrong" smell.

**Landed:** `Expr::Lt`/`Ge`/`Gt` (decimal `<` `>=` `>`) and `DateLt`/`DateGe`/`DateGt` (civil `before` `on_or_after` `after`), each first-class so it round-trips as written. The two duplicated comparator arms became `decimal_comparison`/`date_comparison` helpers parameterised by an `admit` closure - the abstraction the fourth-through-eighth case finally forced. `before`/`after` are matched contextually rather than reserved, because existing examples use them as variable names.


### The explanation engine: rejection as read-side trace interpretation

**Forced by:** the legibility gap. The runtime could say *whether* a transition was admissible; the buyer's question is *why not, and what is still missing*. `propose_with_trace` already carried the failure data, so the work was interpretation, not a new evaluator.

**Landed:** `morpholog_core::explain` returns a structured `Explanation` from the kernel trace - `Admissible`, or a `Rejection` that is a gate (the directly-missing positive claim conjuncts, plus per missing predicate the candidate transformations that assert it via `analysis::transformations_asserting`), an invariant violation, or a kernel error - rendered to deterministic claim-shaped prose or JSON, never NLP (an explanation an auditor relies on must be reproducible and faithful to the exact failing claim, which a probabilistic generator cannot be). The failure-walk gained `unsatisfied_positive_claims`, carried additively on the rejection trace. v0 is one-hop - directly-missing positive claims only; true minimality is a constrained search (abduction), deferred, and the model speaks claims, not "evidence".


### Carbon-credit provenance: the flagship that forced no primitive

**Forced by:** nothing in the kernel - and that is the point. The carbon-credit / certificate-of-origin domain was chosen as the explanation engine's first home because its failure mode is pure legitimacy: a green claim that became official without an admissible, current provenance chain. The test was whether the claim model could carry evidence-provenance with no new primitive.

**Landed:** `carbon_credit_provenance` models the whole chain as claims about claims - `VerifiedMeasurement`, `Attestation`, `Accredited` - gating `issue_credit` on a verified measurement (binding its quantity), an attestation, and a *currently* accredited verifier. Double-counting both directions, single custody, and terminal retirement are invariants; currentness is the verified-revenue `retract`-standing pattern (blocking new issuance while leaving issued credits admitted). No kernel change: the MRV computation stays outside and returns as the admitted `VerifiedMeasurement` quantity, holding the inside/outside boundary. `Expr::Mul` (generation-times-factor) was rejected as crossing that line - the computation is the meter.


### Obligations over time: the outside-coordinator sweep

**Forced by:** the carbon domain's compliance half - a scheme obliges an account to retire enough credits by a deadline. The first worked example with a rule about *time*, and the kernel has no clock by design (no I/O, decidable core).

**Landed:** obligations as ordinary claims (`Obligation(obligation, account, quantity, due_on)`, `ObligationSatisfied`, `ObligationBreached`). `discharge_obligation(obligation, current_date)` admits satisfaction only when the retired total reaches target on or before the deadline (date-aware, so a late retirement cannot quietly satisfy); `sweep_obligation(obligation, current_date)` is the **outside-coordinator tick** - an external scheduler hands "now" in as an argument, never a clock the kernel reads (which would break the decidable core and make admissibility non-reproducible). `obligation_not_both_satisfied_and_breached` keeps the outcomes exclusive. No primitive: the retired total `sum(q | Retired(c, account) and Issued(c, m, q))` confirmed `Sum` handles a join body, and the deadline is the existing `after` comparator.


### `inspect guarantees`: the model's impossibilities, before it runs

**Forced by:** the legibility brief's other half. `explain` answers "why was this rejected?"; a controller asks first "what does this model make impossible?". The carbon flagship, rich with `not(...)` invariants, made it worth its own read.

**Landed:** `morpholog_core::guarantees(program)` returns one `Guarantee` per invariant - the rendered rule, plus a `forbids` clause naming the bad state only for `not(...)` invariants (whose inner expression *is* the forbidden state). `render_guarantees` emits prose; `morpholog inspect guarantees <program>` defaults to prose, `--json` for the structured form. Pure static read over any registered programme, tested across the whole registry so the derivation is general, not handcrafted. Deriving a forbidden state for `implies`/comparator invariants was rejected as semantic interpretation rather than formatting - those carry their rule and no `forbids`, honest over impressive.


### `morpholog explain`: the engine reachable from the command line

**Forced by:** the explanation engine shipped as a library with no way to ask the question from outside Rust. An operational checklist an auditor reads needs a command, not an API the embedder calls.

**Landed:** `morpholog explain <file.morph> <transformation> --args <json> --actor <subject>` - the read-only counterpart of `run`. Same parse/validate front-end and `Transition` codec, but it loads the predicate-scoped pre-state, runs `explain` in-memory, and renders prose (default) or JSON (`--json`). A new public `morpholog_postgres::load_scoped_state` does the read - the sibling of the `propose_against_pg` load, but a plain pooled read, not a SERIALIZABLE transaction, because explaining is a question, not a commit. `.morph`-file source (not the built-in registry) was chosen so an author explains their own model; the verdict does not affect the exit code - zero on both admissible and rejected, only operational failures exit non-zero.


### Collapsing the comparator catalogue into `Expr::Compare`

**Forced by:** not an example - the IR itself. The ordered comparators had grown to eight flat variants (`Le`/`Lt`/`Ge`/`Gt` decimal, `DateLe`/`DateLt`/`DateGe`/`DateGt` date) for what is structurally one operator over one of two ordered domains. The evaluator had already factored them into two helpers; the type never followed, so every `Expr` walker (check, format, validate, analysis, parser) paid an eight-way fan-out - `check` alone enumerated them thirty-eight times. The operator earned being first-class; that had been implemented as eight first-class *variants*, which is a different thing.

**Landed:** one `Expr::Compare { op: CompareOp, domain: OrderedDomain, left, right }`. The operator stays first-class - it still renders and round-trips as written, `>` never becoming `not(<=)` - but the domain is a field, not a fresh variant, so the catalogue stops exploding and a future ordered domain or operator is one row, not four variants across ten files. Zero behaviour change: the surface, every `.morph` file, and every example are untouched (the `dsl` constructors `le`/`date_le`/... kept their names and now build `Compare`), and the round-trip and faithfulness tests prove surface stability. Equality (`Eq`/`Neq`) stayed a separate node, not another `op` on `Compare`: it is kind-agnostic (any two values compare for equality), whereas the ordered comparators are domain-specific (decimal or date). Forcing both into one op-and-domain shape would have to give equality a domain it does not want, or admit a domain-less case the ordered operators are meaningless over - representing combinations that cannot typecheck. Two nodes keep each honest. The same PR did fix the other comparator-family asymmetry, though: `Neq` was lifted from `Term`-only operands to full expressions like `Eq`, so `=` and `!=` are symmetric and `a + 1 != b` now parses - the parser's term-only restriction on `!=` is gone (`in` keeps its own). The `dsl::neq` helper still takes two terms, the common authoring case, and wraps them.


### `.morph` as the single source for the worked examples

**Forced by:** the parser arc's logical conclusion. With every example already parsing faithfully to its registered IR (the `morph_sources_match_registry` test held it there), the hand-built Rust IR in `morpholog-examples` was a second authored copy of each programme - a maintenance tax, and a drift risk the faithfulness test existed only to police.

**Landed:** each example module embeds its `.morph` (`include_str!`), parses it once into a `LazyLock<Program>`, and derives its by-name accessors (`program`, `all_invariants`, individual transformations and invariants, derived claims) from it. The hand-built IR constructors are gone; `morpholog-examples` now depends on `morpholog-surface` (the reverse dev-dependency, for the round-trip test, keeps the cycle dev-only and so allowed). The `.morph` file is the single source of truth - the runnable program *is* the teaching source, so they cannot drift, and the now-vacuous faithfulness test was removed. The public accessor API is unchanged, so the worked-example test call sites across `morpholog-examples` and `morpholog-postgres` did not move; authoring constants (purpose, role, disposition names) stay in the module. The `dsl` keeps its place for hand-authored programmes and core tests - it is just no longer how the examples are written.


### `dsl` -> `ir_builder`: the IR-builder is test tooling, not a language

**Forced by:** the reveal above. Once the examples were authored as `.morph` and the parser became the only thing in the product that builds IR, the `dsl` module was left doing one honest job - constructing IR in tests, including the adversarial and malformed shapes a parser would never emit. The name "DSL" read as a rival language surface, which kept provoking attempts to demote or hide it; one such attempt hit Rust's "two versions of the crate" wall (core's own unit tests cannot use an IR-builder that lives in a crate depending back on core, so it must stay in `morpholog-core`).

**Landed:** `morpholog_core::dsl` renamed to `morpholog_core::ir_builder` - a pure rename, no semantics, still public in core. The name now states the role: a Rust construction kit over the IR, not the Morpholog language. Alongside it, the test layers were named: business and explanation stories are authored in `.morph` (the explain engine's bespoke scenario programmes moved off the builder to inline `.morph`), while invariant-teeth tests that build "the real transformation minus one statement" keep the builder, marked deliberately adversarial. The end-state is not zero builder usage but every remaining use being morally justified - user-facing meaning in `.morph`, kernel-adversarial precision in `ir_builder`.


### Splitting `Expr` into `Prop` and `ValueExpr`

**Forced by:** the deepest conflation left in the IR, and the [constitution](scope-and-ambition.md) that named it. One `Expr` did two jobs - a node yielded a truth-witness in predicate position or a value in value position - so the kernel carried a *dynamic repair for a static mistake*: `find_matches` rejected value-shaped nodes as `NotPredicate`, `eval_value` mirrored it with `NotValue`, and `check.rs`/`validate.rs` re-policed the same boundary statically (`ExpectedValueExpression` / `ExpectedPredicateExpression`). The `.morph`-canonical work made the split tractable: the IR is internal, the parser its sole constructor, and the parser already knows its position.

**Landed:** two sorts. `Prop` (Claim, And, Or, Not, Implies, Exists, Forall, Pre, Eq, Neq, Compare, In) *searches state and produces binding witnesses* - relational, not boolean; `ValueExpr` (Term, Add, Sub, Sum, ValueOf) *computes one value*. They are mutually recursive where it matters: a `Compare`/`Eq`/`Neq` operand is a `ValueExpr`, a `Sum`'s body is a `Prop`. `find_matches(&Prop)` and `eval_value(&ValueExpr)` are now total over their sorts, and the boundary is a Rust type: `EvalError::{NotPredicate, NotValue}` and `ValidationError::{ExpectedValueExpression, ExpectedPredicateExpression}` deleted, unconstructable; the static shape-checks they fed are gone. Kind/type and binding-flow checking stay. The parser splits its productions: `parse_expression -> Prop` (programme bodies are propositions), with a narrower `parse_value_expr` helper, and no `ParsedExpr` union (which would have kept the old one-expression ambiguity at the boundary). `Term` was deliberately *not* split - `Wildcard`-in-value stays a runtime concern - and `Decl`/`Term::Actor` were untouched. Mechanically faithful: every worked example proposes identically and the round-trip test holds; the dead shape-checks were removed only after green.

This sits under the constitution: `Prop`/`ValueExpr` are body grammar, supporting machinery for invariants and transformations. The split *restores* "only invariants and transformations are first-class" by giving their bodies an honest shape, rather than an overloaded `Expr` blob that let anything sit anywhere.

### Opaque newtypes for the kernel's identifiers

**Forced by:** the same conflation the two-sort split fixed, now on the *noun* axis. Every identifier in the IR was a bare `String` - a subject id, a predicate name, a bound variable, a transformation name all the same type - so a function taking `predicate: String` would silently accept a subject. The kernel asserted "subjects are opaque, predicates attach to them, no types over subjects" in prose, not in its types. (Prompted by a survey of how LLM-written Rust drifts: bare `String` where a newtype belongs is the canonical tell.)

**Landed, one opaque newtype per slice** - `From` / `Display` / `as_str` only, no `Deref` / `AsRef` / `Borrow`, so call sites stay explicit. `Subject` came first (the opaque subject primitive, `EvalValue::Subject` / `Value::Subject`), `#[serde(transparent)]` keeping the `{"type":"subject","value":...}` wire shape byte-identical; then `Transition.actor: Subject`, which *deleted* the runtime `matches!(actor, EvalValue::Subject(_))` guard - a non-subject actor is now unrepresentable in the kernel, and the one place a bad actor could still enter (deserialisation) carries the check in the `actor_repr` codec, which keeps the audit/CLI wire shape tagged. Then `Var` for bound-variable names (`Term::Var`, the `forall` / `exists` / `for` binders, `let` names, the `Bindings` keys, the trace's variable-name fields), and finally the name kinds each in their own namespace: `PredicateName`, `IntentName`, `TransformationName`, `InvariantName`. No cross-referenced bare-`String` identifier remains in the IR or the trace; the only `String`s left are genuine rendered text (the `require` / `bind` / invariant-check display strings) and single-use labels that are never looked up across contexts.

Once all the newtypes existed they were near-identical, so the per-kind blocks collapsed into one `opaque_id!` declarative macro (struct + the standard derives + serde-transparent + the `From` / `Display` / `as_str` / symmetric `PartialEq<str>` impls). The `PartialEq<str>` is the load-bearing ergonomic: `name == "Foo"` and `assert_eq!(name, "Foo")` work without re-opening `Deref`, which is what kept the comparison churn off the newtype slices. Construction churn went the same way - hand-built `Transformation` / `Invariant` / `Program` test fixtures route through `ir_builder` builders, so a future IR field touches the builder, not every fixture.

Like the two-sort split, this is the make-the-model-true discipline: push a doctrine the kernel only described into the types, so the compiler enforces it and the runtime stops re-checking. The `Subject` / `actor` slices were hand-propagated by a sub-agent; the rest followed the [refactoring playbook](refactoring-playbook.md) (compiler-worklist plus structural rewrites), which is the bar going forward.

### Trade lifecycle: phase as accumulated claims, forcing no primitive

**Forced by:** nothing in the kernel - and, as with the carbon-credit flagship, that is the point. The trade-lifecycle domain (a commodity trade through capture, confirmation, official-price correction, and settlement) was chosen as the first **external-embedder** target - an ETRM teaching simulator being rebuilt on Morpholog - and the test was whether a multi-phase business lifecycle could be modelled with no new IR shape.

**Landed:** `trade_lifecycle` models the phase as the *accumulation of admitted claims*, never a mutable status field - a trade is "confirmed" because a confirmation claim exists, "settled" because a settlement claim exists. Three decisions worth recording:

- **Lifecycle as claims, not a status enum.** No `TradeStatus(trade, #confirmed)`; the phase is which claims hold, so the history reads itself and as-of replay reconstructs any past phase.
- **Official-price correction is restatement lineage, not mutation.** The confirmation *event* (`TradeConfirmed`) is split from the official price *figure* (`OfficialPrice` plus a retractable `CurrentOfficialPrice` pointer), so an internal price correction moves the pointer and records `Supersedes` without pretending the counterparty re-confirmed - the verified-revenue pattern, applied to the official price. A settlement made under the prior figure stays standing.
- **No declared transformation-parameter kinds forced.** Every parameter flows into a typed claim position, so the check pass infers a total, unambiguous kind for each - the embedder's typed-contract surface (schema generation, friendly args) can ride on inference rather than forcing declared kinds into the IR.

The quantitative gate (`settled_qty <= captured_qty`) reuses `Expr::Compare`; the commodity-scoped authority (`MayConfirm(actor, commodity)`, tied to the trade's own commodity within one `require` conjunction so bindings flow) reuses `Term::Actor`. No kernel change; MTM/pricing stays in the outside compute zone and returns as admitted claims, holding the inside/outside boundary.

### `ValueExpr::Mul` and `Div`: completing the decimal arithmetic

**Forced by:** the borrowing-base example. Asset-backed lending governs a *proportional* limit - the total drawn must stay within an advance rate times the eligible collateral - and reports a *ratio*, the drawn fraction of that collateral. "At most 80% of" is a multiplication; "what fraction is drawn?" is a division. The prior arithmetic surface (`Add`/`Sub`) could express neither.

**Landed:** `ValueExpr::Mul` (exact decimal multiplication, like `Add`/`Sub`) and `ValueExpr::Div` (decimal division; a zero divisor surfaces the new `EvalError::DivisionByZero` rather than producing a value). Surface `*` and `/` parse into them, `*`/`/` binding tighter than `+`/`-`; the formatter parenthesises them like `Add`/`Sub` so round-trip holds. The advance-limit invariant uses `Mul`; the `FacilityUtilisation` read-side derived claim uses `Div`.

This **reopens the carbon example's `Mul` rejection deliberately.** That rejection was right for its case: there `Mul` would have *produced a governed business figure* (the measurement quantity), which belongs in the outside compute zone. The refined line: arithmetic that **expresses an admission gate** (`drawn <= advance_rate * base`) or computes a **read-side projection** (a utilisation ratio, like the trial balance's `Sub`) is inside; arithmetic that **produces a stored governed figure** stays outside. Two disciplines keep `Div` honest: admission gates use the multiplied form with `Mul` so decisions stay exact and never depend on a rounded quotient, and `Div` is confined to read-side projections where a rounded figure is what a reader wants. These are conventions the examples follow, not kernel-enforced restrictions - the kernel does not forbid `Div` inside a gate; enforcing that would need context-sensitive validation not worth its weight here. Decidability is untouched - both are finite and host-function-free. (Deferred: `min`/`max`, which a concentration-cap example would force; unary negation, unforced.)

### `ValueExpr::Min` and `Max`: decimal floor and cap

**Forced by:** the insurance example's per-claim layer. A real policy bounds a claim's payout from both sides at once - the loss above a deductible, capped at a per-claim limit: `min(per_claim_limit, max(0, loss - deductible))`. `max(0, ...)` floors a below-deductible loss at zero; `min(...)` caps it. Neither was expressible with the existing arithmetic.

**Landed:** `ValueExpr::Min` and `Max` (exact decimal minimum/maximum, like the other arithmetic; non-decimal operands surface `TypeMismatch`, no new error mode). Surface form `min(a, b)` / `max(a, b)` - a self-delimiting function shape alongside `sum`, not an infix operator - so the formatter needs no parenthesisation and round-trip holds. The forcing home is an **invariant** (`settlement_within_eligible_payout`), an admission rule, kept purely additive to the insurance example: it only bites when an optional `CoverageTerms` claim is set, so the example's existing settlements (which set none) are vacuously satisfied. With `Mul`/`Div` this rounds out the arithmetic the examples have forced; `Mod` (parity, cyclic rules) is deferred to its own forcing example.

### Collapsing the arithmetic catalogue into `ValueExpr::Arith`

**Prompted by:** the same per-operator churn the comparator collapse cured, now visible on the value sort. Each arithmetic operator (`Add`, `Sub`, `Mul`, `Div`, `Min`, `Max`) was its own `ValueExpr` variant, so every operator added by an example touched the same ten or so match arms across eval, format, check, analysis, validate, and the builders - and the eval arms had drifted into six near-identical copies. The catalogue was exploding the way the comparators had.

**Landed:** one `ValueExpr::Arith { op: ArithOp, left, right }`, mirroring `Prop::Compare { op, domain }`. The operator is a `Copy` field, not a fresh variant; the split that used to be a variant - infix `+ - * /` versus function-shaped `min`/`max` - is now the `ArithOp::is_infix` predicate the printer and surface branch on. Pure internal refactor, zero behaviour change: `ValueExpr` carries no `Serialize`, so there is no wire format to migrate, and the surface, every `.morph` file, and every example are untouched. The `ir_builder` constructors (`add`/`sub`/`mul`/`div`/`min`/`max`) kept their names and now route through one private `arith` helper; the round-trip, eval, format, and example tests are the safety net that the trees are identical. The six eval arms became one that evaluates both operands, matches the decimal pair once, and dispatches on the operator - so a `TypeMismatch` message now reads off the operator's `Debug` (`"Add expects decimal operands"`) rather than a hand-written string per variant. A future operator (`Mod` next) is one `ArithOp` row plus one dispatch line, not a variant across the kernel.

### `ArithOp::Mod`: parity, and the chess remodel that forced it

**Forced by:** the chess example, remodelled. A chessboard square has a colour that is computed, not stored - dark when `file + rank` is even, light when odd. Expressing "the two bishops of a colour stand on opposite-coloured squares" needs `(file + rank) % 2`, and remainder was the one arithmetic operator the examples had not yet forced.

**Landed:** `ArithOp::Mod`, and a square-colour layer on the chess example. The kernel side is the small addition the `Arith{op}` collapse set up: one enum row, one eval dispatch arm (reusing `EvalError::DivisionByZero` for a zero divisor - a remainder by zero is as undefined as a quotient), one `arith_token` row (`%`), and `is_infix` gains it. The surface lexer gains a `Percent` token and the multiplicative parser layer gains `%` alongside `*`/`/`, so the three share one precedence level and `a + b % c` parses as `Add(a, Mod(b, c))`. The collapse paid off exactly as predicted: the per-operator churn that `Mul`/`Div`/`Min`/`Max` each caused is gone.

The remodel replaced the opaque square subject (`#a1`) with `(file, rank)` decimal coordinates - the coordinate *is* the square, no redundant mapping claim - so `PieceAt(file, rank, piece_type, color)`. The forcing rule, `bishops_on_opposite_square_colors`, is a single-snapshot invariant: two different squares holding a same-colour bishop must differ in `(f + r) % 2`. It is model-valid for every reachable position (no promotion here, so a colour never gains a third bishop), and because the model does not otherwise restrict bishop movement, the invariant is what turns away a bishop that would slide onto its partner's colour. The same PR's other arithmetic - the move-count and capture transition invariants - was untouched; `Mod` is the only new expressiveness.

### `Prop::Xor`: a legible exclusive-or

**Prompted by:** legibility, not expressiveness. `(a or b) and not (a and b)` is the standing idiom for "exactly one", but when `a` and `b` are full claim patterns it reads as bookkeeping and buries the intent. This is the only kernel addition so far justified by how a rule *reads* rather than by something an example could not otherwise say.

**Landed:** `Prop::Xor(Box<Prop>, Box<Prop>)`, defined as - and evaluated by lowering to - exactly `(a or b) and not (a and b)`, so its binding semantics are identical to the hand-written form and there is no new evaluation to get wrong. It is binary, not flattened like `And`/`Or`: an n-ary `xor` is ambiguous between "exactly one" and "odd parity", so a chain nests left-associatively rather than forming one node. The surface keyword sits between `and` and `or` in precedence (tighter than `or`, looser than `and`), so `a and b xor c and d` is `(a and b) xor (c and d)` - the natural "exactly one of these two" reading. `find_failing_subexpr` treats it as an opaque rule (like `Or`/`Not`), so an explanation reports `a xor b` whole; the binding-flow check exports a name only if both operands bind it (mirroring the `Or` arm).

**Forcing home:** the KYC example's adjudication fork, which until now modelled only the false-positive path. The confirmed-hit path was completed alongside, so an adjudicated match resolves exactly one way: `MatchAdjudicated(s) implies (ScreeningResult(s, #adjudicated_clear, _, _) xor ScreeningResult(s, #adjudicated_confirmed, _, _))`. The date fields are wildcards on purpose - that keeps the operands ground, so the xor means true exactly-one rather than the looser "exactly-one under some binding" a free variable would give. The invariant's distinctive teeth are the totality half: it rejects an adjudicated marker carrying *neither* disposition, which a plain `not (clear and confirmed)` exclusion would miss. A confirmed hit is made a durable onboarding bar by a companion invariant, closing the back door where an older clean current screening would otherwise admit the customer.

### Trade lifecycle: partial settlements (the ETRM enrichment begins)

**Forced by:** nothing in the kernel - the first step of deepening `trade_lifecycle` toward a realistic ETRM book, before the external embedder is wired. A real commodity trade settles in slices (delivery months, invoices), not in one shot, so `settle_trade`'s one-settlement-per-trade gate was the simplification to lift first.

**Landed:** `settle_trade` may now run more than once per trade, and `TradeSettled` became a settlement *slice* (several per trade). The per-settlement `settled_qty <= captured_qty` invariant became the cumulative `sum(q | TradeSettled(trade, q, _, _)) <= captured_qty` - the running total across slices is what stays in bounds, exactly the aggregate-cap shape the insurance example pins against a policy. Once a settlement is a slice, the settlement id becomes a real idempotency key, and that is split across the two controls the example teaches: a `require not TradeSettled(_, _, settlement_id, _)` gate refuses a replayed id before it can emit a second downstream settlement request (the outbox idempotency boundary), and a `settlement_id_identifies_one_settlement` uniqueness invariant is the backstop for any path the gate cannot see, forbidding two conflicting settlements under one id so the cumulative sum stays honest. No new kernel primitive - `sum`, `<=`, claim uniqueness, and a `not` gate all existed; this is cross-example pattern reuse, the cumulative cap and the gate/invariant split both moving from insurance into the trade domain. The quantity cap stays an invariant, not a gate, so an over-total slice is refused at commit by `settled_quantity_within_captured`.

### Trade lifecycle: effective-dated terms and bitemporal replay

**Forced by:** the second step of the ETRM deepening - a real trade's quantity is re-agreed after the fact, effective from a past date, and the book must still answer "what was effective on date D?" honestly years later. This is the valid-time axis, the counterpart to the transaction-time axis the audit log and `--as-of` already supplied.

**Landed:** the quantity moved off `TradeCaptured` (now immutable identity - commodity, direction) onto a versioned `TradeTerms(trade, version_id, quantity, delivery_period, effective_from)`, each version carrying the date its quantity takes force. `amend_trade_terms` admits a later version effective from a date that may be backdated relative to the commit; the original stays on the record, `TradeTermsSupersedes` records lineage, and the new effective date must be distinct. `TradeSettled` gained an `effective_on` business date, and the generic `Supersedes` split into `OfficialPriceSupersedes` and `TradeTermsSupersedes` (two versioned things should not share one predicate, so the two linear-chain invariants stay independent). The mirror of the official-price split: the immutable event (`TradeCaptured`, the confirmation) is separated from the amendable figure (`TradeTerms`, `OfficialPrice`).

**Forced no primitive - the headline.** The plan budgeted for a possible "effective-time query primitive" (a latest-fact-on-or-before-D lookup) if the cap turned unreadable. It did not. "The terms in force on `d`" is the *not-a-later-one* pattern - `TradeTerms(trade, _, qty, _, ef) and ef on_or_before d and not (exists later_ef: ... later_ef on_or_before d and later_ef after ef)` - built from `on_or_before`, `after`, `exists`, and `not`, all of which existed. The cap became a running total *by effective date*: at each settlement effective date `d`, `sum(s | TradeSettled(trade, s, _, _, sd) and sd on_or_before d) <= qty` where `qty` is the terms in force on `d`. The empirical question - would a `sum` body carrying an outer-bound date comparison (`sd on_or_before d`) and an outer-bound `qty` validate - resolved cleanly on the first `check`. So effective time is not a kernel feature; it is a modelling pattern over claims that already carried dates (forced into the kernel by the clinical-trial example). This raises the bar on the kernel-ahead-of-example deviation: an entire bitemporal capability landed as pure modelling.

**Bitemporality demonstrated, not built.** The two axes compose without machinery: the valid axis is the `effective_from`/`effective_on` dates on claims (retroactively writable), the transaction axis is the audit log replayed by `reconstruct_state_at`. The PG test reads `TermsTimeline` as-of two transitions and gets two truthful answers to one fixed effective-date question (100 before the backdated amendment is recorded, 120 after) - no valid-time columns, no temporal database, the floor the prior-art doc draws against XTDB-as-substrate. The example's two former deferred notes ("effective time as a separate axis", "trade re-versioning") retired; a same-effective-date correction (needing a supersession tiebreak like the official price) is the remaining deferred shape. Multi-LLM review then closed two holes the running cap exposed, both with existing kinds (still no primitive): `settled_date_has_effective_terms` (a settle gate plus backstop invariant) so a slice effective before the first terms version cannot escape the cap by vacuity, and positivity invariants on terms and settled quantities so a negative slice cannot make room under the running sum for an over-large positive one.

### `IntentDecl` and `PredicateDecl`: structurally similar, deliberately not unified

**Considered:** collapsing `PredicateDecl` and `IntentDecl` into a single `Decl` struct. Both carry only a name and a `Vec<ArgDecl>`; `VocabularyKind` already tags them in lookups (`check.rs`) and in `ValidationError` variants; the structural identicality is real.

**Decision:** keep them as two distinct structs. The unification would not pay off. The name fields use distinct opaque newtypes (`PredicateName` and `IntentName`, locked in by the identifier-newtype arc), so a generic `Decl<N>` would carry the same parameter at every use site without ever ranging over both, and a `Decl { name: String }` would erase the newtype distinction the kernel just paid to add. `Program` keeps `predicates: Vec<PredicateDecl>` and `intents: Vec<IntentDecl>` as separate fields regardless, because the two vocabularies describe distinct kinds of thing (admitted claim shapes versus outbox effect shapes), and that separation is honest at the type level. Subtraction over churn: the two-struct shape carries no maintenance tax that unifying would remove. `Term::Actor` was reconsidered in the same pass and left as a `Term` variant - it is a capability boundary, and the reserved-binding alternative offered no real simplification for the risk of weakening that boundary.

### The first embedder-facing kernel surface: per-transformation argument schemas

**Forced by:** the external ETRM embedder, the next thread of work. The model-enrichment arc on `trade_lifecycle` is the substrate; the embedder consumes it from outside the workspace. The smallest surface that lets it do so honestly is "given a validated programme and a transformation name, tell me what the outside world must supply": parameter kinds in declaration order, encoded for a non-Rust caller.

**Landed:** the analysis accessor and a JSON Schema adapter. `analysis::transformation_param_kinds(program, name) -> Result<Vec<(Var, ParamKind)>, AnalysisError>` is the kernel piece - the resolved input contract. `schema::transformation_arg_schema(program, name) -> Result<serde_json::Value, AnalysisError>` renders it as JSON Schema (Draft 2020-12): Subject as opaque-uuid string, Decimal as strictly-patterned string (no leading zeros, no bare `+`, no trailing dot), Date as ISO civil date, Bool as boolean, Collection as untyped array, Polymorphic and Unconstrained as typeless properties whose description carries the state. The boundary is deliberate: **the kernel exports the inferred input contract, not a client protocol.** A future embedder wanting an OpenAPI component or a Python dataclass would render the same `ParamKind` result a different way; the truth of the analysis is exported once, the encodings are adapters.

**Forced no IR change - the headline.** The memory's pre-embedder gate held: every parameter of every transformation in `trade_lifecycle` flows into a typed claim or intent position, so the existing static-check inference is total - no parameter resolves to `Polymorphic` or `Unconstrained`, no declared transformation-parameter kinds were forced into the IR. The integration test pins this on the full model: a regression that re-introduces an inference gap would fail loudly on the next change.

**Sibling walker, not re-run.** The static checker's existing `check_program` walks `Require` (and `Or` branches, `Sum` / `For` bodies) in a *cloned* scope - the right semantics for the runtime binding-flow doctrine, because match bindings inside a gate must not export to later statements. But that semantics is wrong for an external input contract: a parameter used only inside `require` is still externally supplied, and its slot still observes a concrete kind. Authority-gated transformations (the `require can_approve(actor, asset)` shape) make this the most-likely real failure mode - and `trade_lifecycle`'s `settle_trade` lives there: `settlement_id`, `official_price_id`, and `effective_on` are observed only inside `require` gates. So the accessor uses a new walker, alongside the checker, that accumulates kind observations across the union of every visited position in a flat environment that is never cloned. It tracks all variables (not only parameters) so intermediate `let` / bind / quantifier vars can carry cross-position observations through to a parameter, then projects parameters out in declaration order at the end. The regression test pinning this trap (`param_used_only_inside_require_resolves_to_concrete`) is the must-have a code reviewer should look for; the trade-lifecycle integration test exercises it on `settle_trade`.

**`ParamKind` carries `Concrete` / `Polymorphic` / `Unconstrained` / `Ambiguous`, not `Option<PredicateArgKind>`.** Each variant maps to genuinely different embedder behaviour, so collapsing any pair loses a distinction the embedder presents differently. A `Polymorphic` parameter flows through `PredicateArgKind::Any` slots (the declaration-time escape hatch) and the embedder should accept input but flag that the kernel cannot narrow it; an `Unconstrained` parameter is never observed in any kind-bearing position and the embedder should surface it as dead-or-broken; an `Ambiguous` parameter is observed at more than one distinct concrete kind across cloned scopes the checker hides (typically `Or` branches the runtime picks between), and the embedder should render it disjunctively (JSON Schema `anyOf` in the adapter that landed). The first review of this PR caught a bug here: an earlier collector refined into one `InferredKind` per variable and silently dropped a conflicting later observation, which would have made the schema lie about transformations that legitimately accept disjunctive input shapes. The fix accumulates a *set* of observed kinds per variable and projects multi-kind sets to `Ambiguous(Vec<PredicateArgKind>)` in deterministic declaration order. Reporting one concrete kind is a lie; refusing to emit a schema is too strict; collapsing to `Polymorphic` hides the diagnostic.

**Eq / Neq cross-refinement deliberately omitted.** The walker does not pin a bare-variable operand of `Eq` / `Neq` from a literal on the other side. Adding it would require re-implementing kind inference over every `ValueExpr` shape - a meaningful jump in complexity for a case real models do not exercise. Parameters flow through claim / intent arg positions; the trade-lifecycle integration test confirms total inference without cross-refinement. An example that genuinely needs it becomes the witness that forces it back in - matching the kernel-primitive-plus-forcing-example doctrine.

### `Value::Timestamp` and `Value::Duration`: the time arc opens

**Forced by:** `examples/12_laytime_demurrage/` - voyage chartering's argument about minutes. Laytime is a *time allowance*; demurrage is the priced excess past it; the Statement of Facts is a minute-by-minute log both sides audit. Modelling it honestly needs exact instants (the NOR tendered at 14:00Z), exact spans (six hours of turn time, forty-eight hours of allowance), instants shifting by spans (commencement = NOR + turn time), instants differencing into spans (an interval's length from its ends), and spans summing against an allowance. None of that is expressible over `Decimal` and civil `Date` without lying about what the values are.

**Landed:** `Value::Timestamp(String)` / `EvalValue::Timestamp(jiff::Timestamp)` (RFC 3339, `@2026-10-24T14:00:00Z` extending the date sigil) and `Value::Duration(String)` / `EvalValue::Duration(jiff::SignedDuration)` (ISO 8601 exact time units, the deliberately boring `duration(PT6H)` constructor - no quotes, no literal DSL). The arithmetic rule matrix is enforced twice: `NoArithRule` at authoring time names both operand kinds when no rule exists; evaluation refuses the same pairs at runtime. `Sum` became type-driven (decimal or duration, never mixed). Two comparator families, all contextual identifiers: `at_or_before` / `strictly_before` / `at_or_after` / `strictly_after` over instants, `no_longer_than` / `shorter_than` / `no_shorter_than` / `longer_than` over spans.

**Zone-less by design - the headline.** A `Timestamp` is an exact UTC instant; the kernel knows nothing of timezones, DST, or local calendars. The 25-hour day when the clocks fall back, the "weather working day" with local boundaries - that interpretation is *domain knowledge*, and the plan (the laytime example's next stage) is to admit it as claims from an external calendar authority (`PortDay(port, local_date, starts_at, ends_at, tzdb_version)`-shaped), never to consult a timezone database hidden in the runtime. The forcing argument is replay determinism: a tzdb is data that changes over time, and a computation whose answer depends on which tzdb the runtime happened to ship cannot honestly replay. External interpretive facts become claims, not runtime assumptions.

**`SignedDuration`, not `Span`.** jiff's `Span` carries calendar units (months, years) whose lengths depend on context the kernel would have to guess. Exact seconds only; a "month of laytime" is not a thing a charterparty says.

**The empty-sum landmine, pinned rather than papered over.** The empty `Sum` evaluates to decimal zero - the only backward-compatible choice - so a duration aggregate over zero matches would compare decimal-to-duration and error. The honest answer is a modelling pattern, not a coercion: the laytime clock's commencement admits a zero-length seed interval, so the count is never a sum over nothing. The `time_values` suite pins both the landmine and the seed; the alternative (a kind-annotated `Sum` or a polymorphic zero) waits for an example the pattern cannot serve.

**Demurrage is not an invariant violation.** The first modelling instinct - `counted no_longer_than allowed` as an invariant - is wrong: running over the allowance is the normal commercial story, the very thing demurrage prices. The model's invariants are the bookkeeping truths (an interval's recorded length equals the gap between its ends; counted time follows commencement; the clock starts once), and the excess is a *derived* claim, floored at zero with the duration `max` the same example forced. Choosing what NOT to make an invariant is part of the modelling discipline this example teaches.

### `Value::Quantity` and `PredicateArgKind::Quantity(Unit)`: units land as labels

Forced by the laytime example's third stage (`examples/12_laytime_demurrage/`): the cargo book in tonnes against a vessel's capacity, the demurrage rate and its settlements in dollars against what the delay is worth.

**Units are contractual labels on exact decimals, not physical dimensions - the headline.** A `Decimal[USD]` is an exact decimal whose declared unit must match before arithmetic or comparison is allowed; the kernel knows nothing else. No registry, no aliases, no SI semantics, no compound symbols: `USD/day` is a predicate field name and a formula (`daily * (excess / duration(PT24H))`), never a unit, which keeps the whole dimensional-analysis rabbit hole (is `ton` short, long, or metric? is `MWh` energy while `MW` is power?) outside the kernel. Conversions are domain knowledge with provenance and time - whose table, as of when? - so they enter as admitted claims when an example forces one, the exact doctrine that keeps tzdb lookups out of the runtime. The unit joins the opaque-id newtypes.

**The algebra is the smallest one the example forced.** Same-unit add / subtract / min / max / sum / compare; a bare decimal scales a quantity; the ratio of two same-unit quantities is a bare decimal (the borrowing-base shape); and `Duration / Duration` is a dimensionless decimal computed from exact integer-nanosecond inputs by decimal division (terminating ratios like 132h/24h = 5.5 are exact; a repeating ratio carries Decimal's 28-digit precision) - which is how "per day" stays a formula. Money settled off a ratio will eventually want an explicit rounding/quantisation rule; that is a domain decision to model as claims or gates, never a hidden kernel default. Nothing produces a unit that was not already written down. Enforced twice, the time-arc pattern: the static matrix at authoring time, the eval matrix at runtime - and a third walker lesson emerged: `Mul`/`Div` stopped being decimal-only, so the checker and the schema collector route every operator through the matrix, keeping a bare-decimal default only when NEITHER side is determinable, because **a unit can be declared, never inferred**.

**The wire carries no unit.** Kind-pinned declarations (`Decimal[USD]`) mean the named codec accepts and returns the bare amount - the declaration is the single source of truth, and an embedder's JSON does not change shape when a parameter gains a unit. The schema says it twice: `x-morpholog-unit` for machines, the unit in the description for the form generators that ignore extensions. Only the self-describing tagged codec spells the unit out.

**Diagnostics print the declaration syntax.** `no arithmetic rule for Decimal[USD] Add Decimal[t]` - the unit always renders, never a unit-erased "Quantity", via one `Display` impl the formatter and the validation errors share. The point of the feature is to catch money-meets-tonnes mistakes; an error that hides the units would forfeit it.

**The seed pattern is now a pattern.** Quantity aggregates inherit empty-sum-is-decimal-zero, so the cargo book opens with a `0 t` parcel and the money book with a `0 USD` settlement, exactly as the laytime clock seeds its zero-length interval. Two occurrences make the modelling idiom official; a typed aggregate identity waits for an example the seed cannot serve.

### Example 13 (EU AI Act Article 12) and `inspect controls`: the zero-kernel statute

The thirteenth worked example models Articles 12 and 14(5) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 - automatic record-keeping for high-risk AI, and two-distinct-person verification before acting on a biometric identification. Its design-history headline is what it forced: **nothing in the kernel or the surface.** Authority grant/revoke (the approval-controls shape), admission-time validity windows (the clinical-trial shape), standing granted by verification (verified revenue), and exact instants (laytime) composed directly into a statute. The eight-beat demo script - five refusals and the happy path - ran green on the first build with no language change. That outcome is the result worth writing up, not a footnote: it is the load-bearing evidence that the four governance patterns the repo accumulated across finance and energy examples are domain-independent.

**The two-person rule is both a gate and an invariant, deliberately.** The authority check ("the verifier holds live oversight") lives in the `verify_match` gate, so revoking an overseer never reaches back into history - example 04's lesson. The distinctness requirement ("two verifications by different people") lives in an invariant, which is sound *only because* verification records are append-only in this model: nothing retracts them, so a decision admitted under two distinct verifications keeps them forever. The honest note for the corrections stage this repo keeps building toward: a verification later found fraudulent will need the exception-claims pattern (a typed in-repair claim with audit standing), not a retraction, precisely to keep that invariant safe.

**No clock, by design.** Every timestamp is supplied by the proposer and judged by the gates; the model never reads "now". That is what makes the statutory record deterministic - it replays identically in front of a regulator - and it is a point AI-governance reviewers ask about, so the README says it outright.

**`inspect controls`, forced by the example's own artefact.** The most citable thing the example produces is the statute-clause-to-rule-name mapping table. Hand-maintaining it would be exactly the drift the project exists to prevent, so the example forced the legibility surface that regenerates it from the rules: a control matrix pairing every transformation's `require`/`bind` preconditions (what must be true before each action, with the predicates each consults) with the invariant guarantees (what can never be true). It is the gate-side complement to `inspect guarantees`' invariant-side view, completing the two questions an auditor asks. Pure deterministic rendering over the IR - no kernel primitive, reusing `predicates_referenced_by_prop` and the `guarantees` machinery - which is why it rode in the same PR as the example rather than waiting: the example is its forcing case, the way the carbon model forced `explain`.

### `define`: named propositions, the language's second authoring tier

**Forced by:** the worked examples themselves, three ways at once. The clinical-trial randomisation gate was one ~20-line conjunction with no way to name its sub-conditions (the file's own comments apologised for the bulk). The AI Act example carried the statute's two-distinct-verifiers proposition verbatim in two places - the decision gate and the permanent invariant - with only discipline keeping them identical. The trade lifecycle's "terms in force on a date" was the least readable invariant in the repo. One missing construct under all three strains: a way to declare a recurring condition once, under the name the business uses for it.

**Landed:** `Definition { name, parameters, body }` on `Program`; `Prop::Defined { name, args }` as the call; surface `define name(params): body`, with calls spelled exactly like claim references and resolved by name once the whole programme is collected (so a call may precede its definition, and round-trip holds for free). The load-bearing decisions:

- **Relational substitution with projection, not macro expansion and not a closure.** The body evaluates under a frame carrying only the parameters - ground arguments pre-bind theirs, unbound ones act as generators - and each body match projects parameter values back onto the call's arguments. Hygiene is absolute: the body cannot see caller scope (an adversarial ir_builder test pins that a free name errors rather than captures), and the caller cannot see body internals. Projection deduplicates, so a call yields each distinct argument-binding witness once and internal multiplicity cannot double-count inside a `sum` - the test suite pins the contrast (the call counts 1 where its inlined body counts 2).
- **Context-free bodies.** `actor` and `pre(...)` inside a body are validation errors; a definition means the same thing in a gate as in an invariant. The actor passes in as an ordinary call argument (the clinical gate's delegation check), resolving at the call site; a call wrapped in `pre(...)` works because the context swap applies to the body's evaluation.
- **One canonical home for the machinery.** A `definitions` module owns name lookup, the reference graph (cycles refused; the topo order shared by kind inference and the depth budget, which charges each call its callee's expanded depth), and the resolution pass - so the evaluator, the failure walk, the missing-claims walk, and the static checks cannot drift on what a call expands to.
- **Every walker transitive, as a red line.** Predicate-scoped loading descends through calls on both read paths (a PG test proves a gate behind two definition levels still loads its claims); the checker infers per-parameter kinds and generator-capability callees-first and validates call sites against them (a use-only parameter demands a bound argument - the runtime-faithful rule); the schema collector observes call arguments against inferred kinds, keeping embedder schemas total; `inspect controls` lists the predicates a gate consults through its named conditions.
- **Two-level diagnostics.** A failing call renders the named condition first, the responsible body conjunct second, and the explanation engine descends through bodies - the AI Act test pins that a refused decision names `two_distinct_prior_verifications` AND still lists the missing `MatchVerified` with `verify_match` as its supplier.

**Considered and rejected:** inline expansion at parse (would have cost the trace and explain the business name - the construct's main payoff); unifying with derived claims (related but distinct: `define` names a proposition searched with the caller's bindings, `derived` enumerates claims over a domain - collapsing them would conflate search with enumeration); letting bodies see caller scope (convenient and poisonous - every call site would need the body's internals to be read safely).

**The audit-honesty note** rides in `runtime-semantics.md`: a definition edit changes invariant meaning without changing invariant text, so the programme hash, not the invariant list alone, names the rules in force.

### Claim disciplines: the doctrine moves onto the predicate declarations

**Forced by:** the census the strategic review commissioned. The worked examples carried 22 hand-written uniqueness invariants (~80 lines of bookkeeping with no business content), four currentness pointers re-deriving the same singleton and no-fork rules, and - the sharpest finding - models whose invariant-safety RESTED on append-only-ness asserted only in comments (the verified-revenue records, the approval records, the AI Act's two-person rule, which is sound as an invariant only because nothing retracts a verification). Doctrine the runtime could not see.

**Landed:** four declaration clauses - `unique by (fields)`, `append only`, `current pointer by (fields)`, `superseded via L` - with one enforcement story: lower to ordinary generated invariants where a state rule is needed, enforce statically where that is cheaper, record metadata either way. The load-bearing decisions:

- **Full-agreement uniqueness.** `unique by` means the keys determine the whole claim (the SQL-UNIQUE reading); several clauses may coexist. Three examples were thereby deliberately strengthened, each flagged in its comments and pinned by a test against state the old rule passed: the trade terms' effective date now determines the whole version, a voyage's NOR is one tender event in that model, an identification match determines its whole decision record. Partial-agreement syntax was rejected - no surviving use case, and an id carrying two outcomes is a state no model wants.
- **`append only` is a static check, not an invariant.** Retraction only happens through a `retract` statement, so the authoring-time ban (checked through nested `for` bodies) is complete and free at runtime. No escape hatch: corrections are supersessions or exception claims.
- **The pointer tier decomposes.** `current pointer by` IS `unique by` plus the retractable-pointer class; `superseded via` is no-fork on the lineage - which is itself `unique by` the prior field, under the `(successor, prior)` convention the examples had already established - plus lineage-is-append-only. **No-fork only**: joins and cycles are deliberately outside its claim. The pointer-has-figure invariants (the official price's) stay hand-written; a figure-predicate reference in the clause awaits its own design.
- **Materialised, prepended, inspectable.** Generated invariants land in `Program.invariants` with `origin: Discipline`, BEFORE the authored ones, so a proposal violating both a discipline and a knock-on rule is refused with the root cause named (the insurance duplicate-policy test caught the tail-position attribution naming `headroom_consumed_by_payment` instead). They are enforced, scoped, audited, and rendered like any rule; `guarantees` carries `from:` provenance back to the clause; the formatter omits them so round-trip holds and the hash covers declarations plus the invariants they imply. Virtual (non-materialised) enforcement was rejected as a hidden layer every caller could forget; validation also refuses hand-built IR whose declared discipline was never lowered.
- **Boring names are load-bearing.** `{snake(P)}_unique_by_{fields}` appears in rejection reasons and `invariants_checked`; the pointer lowering reuses the same scheme rather than inventing a semantic name.

**Considered and rejected:** preserving the authored invariant names via an `as` clause (surface for sentiment - the provenance line serves the traceability need); a keyless global-singleton form (refused as vacuous under set semantics; no example forces it); making disciplines a general rule-template mechanism (the boundary sentence stands: properties of claim shapes - boring, deterministic, generated, visible, and few).

### The lint tier, opened by the gate-vs-invariant hint

**Forced by:** the disciplines PR's own promise. Declaring append-only and current-pointer classes made the oldest doctrine in the repo - authority and currentness checks belong in gates, never invariants - mechanically nameable, and the roadmap had long carried a `--strict` hint layer with no occupant to force it.

**Landed:** a `Lint` surface in core distinct from `ValidationError` on purpose: an error means the programme cannot mean what it says; a lint means it says something that is usually, but not always, a mistake. `check` prints findings to stderr as hints and passes; `--strict` promotes them to errors; stdout stays silent either way, preserving the script contract. The first lint fires on an invariant whose antecedent positively references an append-only predicate and whose consequent positively requires a current-pointer predicate - retracting the pointer would make already-admitted records violate the rule. The walk is polarity-aware (a negated pointer consequent gets STRONGER under retraction and stays silent; a nested implication flips its left side; `sum` bodies tolerate zero matches and contribute nothing) and descends through defined calls. **Forward direction only**: the reverse - a pointer required to name an existing record, like the trade lifecycle's pointer-has-a-figure rule - is correct doctrine and silent.

**The evidence line:** every worked example is lint-clean, pinned by a cross-example test. The disciplines sweep declared the doctrine without introducing the shape the lint names - and the KYC example's onboarding rule, which has the pointer-consequent shape deliberately (continuous compliance is its intent), stays clean precisely because `OnboardedCustomer` is not declared append-only. That deliberate reading is why the finding is a hint, not an error.

### `run --batch`: the import shape, and an exit code that tells the truth

**Forced by:** the external embedder's import path - the shape the roadmap had pinned since the Glasshouse evaluation (rows in, a receipt per row, each its own SERIALIZABLE commit) and the first throughput lever the bench's contend axis names (one invocation amortises the spawn/parse/validate floor across the file).

**Landed:** NDJSON rows naming their own transformation and actor, decoded by the same two codecs as the flags through the same shared decoder, proposed through the same adapter calls, emitting the same envelopes - plus a `row` line number, so the batch contract cannot drift from the pinned single-run contract. Two decisions worth recording: a malformed row is a receipt, not a process failure (each row is independent; the rows after a corrupt line still run), and **the exit code tells the import's truth** - zero whenever every row was processed, because partial admission is an import's normal outcome and the receipts are the result; non-zero is reserved for operational failure. The contrast with single `run` (exit 1 on rejection) is documented in the embedder contract rather than smoothed over.

### Source-located diagnostics: every finding points at the line

**Forced by:** the asymmetry every author hit - parse errors arrived with ariadne carets while validation errors, lint hints, and runtime rejections said *what* but never *where*. The runtime-semantics doc had explicitly marked span-threading as a separate decision; the Glasshouse authoring workflow (models far larger than the worked examples) made that decision due.

**Landed:** spans survive parsing instead of being dropped. The parser had always captured a span per declaration in its intermediate `RawProgram` and discarded them when building the final `Program`; the `SourceMap` keeps them - plus one span per top-level transformation-body statement - and `parse_program_with_sources` returns it beside the IR, with `parse_program` as a discarding wrapper so the existing callers are untouched. The load-bearing decisions:

- **The map lives beside the IR, never inside it.** The kernel stays source-agnostic (a `Program` can be hand-built or deserialised); the one position the kernel carries is an index, not a span - `ValidationContext::Transformation` records which body statement a finding was made in, and the surface resolves the index to a span. The finding-to-source lookups (`span_for_error`, `span_for_lint`) live with the map in `morpholog-surface`, where a future LSP reuses them.
- **Declaration + top-level-statement granularity.** Sub-expression spans are deliberately deferred to the kernel arc's shared-fold refactor; a statement nested in a `for` inherits the `for`'s span. The granularity a worked author actually needs first is "which line do I open".
- **Unresolvable findings degrade, never block.** A finding against a generated discipline invariant or a hand-built name resolves to no span and renders as the previous plain line; `check --json` carries it with severity and message only.
- **The runtime rejection location is a stderr courtesy, not an envelope change.** `run` resolves the backticked invariant in a rejection reason to its declaration and prints `rule at <file>:<line>:<col> (<name>)` to stderr, single-run only - every pinned stdout envelope stays byte-identical, and batch receipts stay the whole machine contract.
- **Spans end at the declaration's own last token.** A `Dedent` is anchored at the end of the block it closes, not at the start of the token that revealed it, so a declaration ending in an indented block (discipline clauses, a transformation body) never swallows the blank lines and comments before the next declaration. Surfaced in review as a cosmetic overshoot and fixed at the layout pass rather than recorded as a TODO, because carets are trust-sensitive: an over-wide span reads as a wrong span.

**Considered and rejected:** spans inside the IR (every hand-built and deserialised programme would carry dead weight, and the kernel would grow a source dependency it has no use for); rendering the full teaching-length message under the carets as well as in the header (ariadne's label now just points); a `--locations` flag on `run`'s stdout envelope (stderr costs nothing and the envelope contract stays frozen).

### The client is a projection too: `generate python-client` and `schema --result`

**Forced by:** convergence, the same bar that forced `--named`. Two independent Python embedders - Glasshouse and the worked embedder example - hand-wrote the same interface layer: the same codecs from the codec contract, the same envelope models from the envelope contract, the same subprocess adapter from the CLI contract, the same manifest-driven models. None of those lines was embedder-specific; all of them could drift. The doctrine already named the resolution: `.morph` is the truth and the schema, request, and response are projections of it - the client was the one projection still hand-maintained downstream. The same argument that embedded the schema in the binary for `init` applies: an embedder should speak exactly the contract its binary speaks.

**Landed:** `morpholog generate python-client <file.morph> --out <dir>`, plus the `schema --result` outcome-envelope contract it consumes. The load-bearing decisions:

- **Static templates emitted verbatim; only the models are generated.** The programme-independent modules (codecs, envelopes, adapter) are real `.py` files in the repo, unit-tested in place and emitted byte-for-byte - the tested file IS the emitted file, and determinism comes free. Only `models.py` and the stamped `__init__.py` vary per programme.
- **One sample set holds three artefacts together.** The golden envelope files are byte-equal to the binary's real serialization (the Rust contract test constructs real outcomes, real `explain()` verdicts, a real outbox row), structurally validated against the pinned `result.json`, and loaded by the generated client's own envelope tests. The binary, the schema document, and the emitted client cannot drift apart without a test naming it. The seed embedder's golden captures were deliberately NOT inherited - they predated the batch contract - only their semantics were.
- **Models read `ParamKind` and the declarations, never the JSON fragments.** The schema module's own doc had already named a Python dataclass as "a separate adapter built from the same analysis result"; the generator is that adapter. Exhaustive kind matches mean a new kind breaks the build, not the output.
- **Stdlib-only, frozen dataclasses, a declared and CI-enforced 3.10 floor.** Any library choice would couple a Rust project to that library's release cadence - the treadmill the no-PyPI-package decision exists to avoid. The floor is enforcement, not preference: the CI job runs at exactly 3.10, which is what catches the floor-version traps (the `Z`-suffix `fromisoformat` gap, scientific `Decimal` rendering, the six-digit fraction limit) the templates handle by name.
- **Whole-run refusal.** Non-concrete parameter kinds (the `--args-named` rule), Duration / Collection / Any fields, Python-keyword and un-emittable names: every finding listed, nothing written. Refusing beats mangling, which would silently divorce field names from wire names. The laytime example is refused by name - the honest consequence of the kind floor until an embedder forces those models.
- **The adapter decodes, the contract names.** Surfaced by the worked-example rewrite itself: payload models construct from the decoded positional values the adapter's outbox row already carries, not from tagged wire values - one decode, owned by one layer.

**Considered and rejected:** Pydantic emission (the seed was Pydantic-based; embedders that want richer boundary types build them FROM the generated types - Glasshouse's own layering conclusion); a stamped header on the static modules (would break tested-file-is-emitted-file for zero information the `__init__` stamps don't carry); inheriting the seed's golden captures (stale against the post-batch contract); a `jsonschema` dev-dependency for the contract test (a large audit tree for what a forty-line structural walker pins).

### inspect coverage: which of these rules has ever actually done work?

**Forced by:** the bitemporal-vacuity lesson made a real wound of it - an effective-time invariant went silently vacuous when no governing claim existed - and every independent literature survey ranked rule-quality reporting among the highest-leverage moves. The verification arc's opener, and deliberately kernel-free: replay plus the polarity machinery the lint tier already owned.

**Landed:** `morpholog inspect coverage <file.morph>` - the audit log replayed through a coverage tracker, reporting per invariant whether its condition ever matched anything, and per transformation whether it was ever used. The load-bearing decisions:

- **The verdicts are bounded by what committed history can honestly show, and the report says so.** The audit log records only committed transitions - a rejected proposal leaves no row - so "this rule refused N proposals" is structurally unknowable. The three verdicts that survive: *fired* (an antecedent bound a real witness, with count and first/last transition ids), *never fired* (dynamically vacuous - the headline), and *always on* (prohibitions and bare comparisons hold over every committed state by construction; their work is invisible here). The two absent verdicts are named in the report's own legend with their reasons: *constrained* awaits a rejection log; *dead antecedent* is static analysis, the offline-oracle tier. Defining this precisely before implementation was itself a review request on the planning round.
- **Shape classification reuses the lint tier's polarity walker - which the review made honest.** An invariant is implication-shaped when `collect_implications` finds positive-polarity `Implies` nodes, descending through quantifiers, conjunctions, and - the review's second catch - definition calls, per the every-walker-transitive red line; the fix strengthened the gate-vs-invariant lint in the same stroke (an implication hidden behind a named condition is now linted wherever it is spelled, pinned on both consumers). Everything else is always-on. Every generated discipline invariant is implication-shaped, so the disciplines sweep made the whole uniqueness/pointer layer measurable for free, provenance carried.
- **The delta prune is the cost model - and `pre(...)` antecedents are exempt from it.** An antecedent is re-evaluated only when a transition's asserted/retracted predicates intersect its footprint (`predicates_referenced_by_prop`, transitive through definitions); state snapshots are taken only then. A pre-reading antecedent's firing opportunity lags the delta by one transition (the claim asserted at T sits in the PRE-state only from T+1), so pruning it by the current delta would skip exactly the transition where it first binds - the review's first catch, fixed by never pruning those entries and pinned by a regression test whose firing lands on a delta-mismatched transition. Transition invariants get the previous state (the empty state before the first transition), so they evaluate instead of erroring.
- **The replay reads under `SERIALIZABLE READ ONLY DEFERRABLE`** - the pointer recorded in prior-art from the PostgreSQL SSI papers, taken at its first opportunity: a guaranteed-serializable snapshot with zero SSI footprint for a long analytical read over a live system.
- **Coverage measures today's rules over recorded history.** The `invariants_checked` audit column (name+version per transition) is the substrate for a later "when did this rule enter service" tier, deliberately unconsulted in v0. Transformations seen in history but absent from the current programme are flagged as drift, never dropped.

**The evidence line:** the worked-embedder lifecycle replayed through coverage tells a coherent story unprompted - every uniqueness rule fires exactly where its claims first appear, `amend_trade_terms - never used` pairs with its supersession rule's NEVER FIRED, and the settlement rules fire exactly once. A report whose findings cross-confirm is the legibility the arc exists for.

**Considered and rejected:** reporting prohibitions as never-fired (a dishonest conflation - holding with nothing to match is not the same as holding because nothing was ever at stake; they get their own verdict); evaluating against delta-only bindings (an antecedent can join new claims against old state); counting binding witnesses rather than transitions (multiplicity is noise at the auditor's altitude - "fired in 2 transitions" reads, "bound 17 witnesses" misleads).

### The rejection log: refusals become operational evidence

**Forced by:** coverage's own legend. The committed-history bound left `constrained` named-but-absent - "this rule refused N proposals" was structurally unknowable because a rejection rolls back and vanishes - and the roadmap carried the rejection log as the substrate that would lift it. The diagnostics round had separately flagged that rejection reasons wanted structure (a rule name to resolve, not display text to parse); this PR is where that note was due.

**Landed:** `morpholog.rejections` - one row per refused proposal, written by the PG adapter; coverage's `constrained` verdict counted from it; `inspect rejections` to list it. The load-bearing decisions:

- **Recorded after the rollback, and the doctrine follows from that ordering.** A refusal's record cannot live inside the transaction that refused, so it is one autocommit insert AFTER the rollback: at-most-once (a crash between rollback and insert loses the record), operational evidence, never tamper-grade, never consulted by any gate or invariant. The audit log remains the only legitimacy-grade record, and every consumer's prose says so - coverage calls the refusal counts a floor, not a census. Default-on, no flag; a failed insert is an operational error, never a fake rejected envelope.
- **Structure at the source, string at the boundary.** The kernel's `Outcome::Rejected` now carries a `RejectionReason` enum (invariant name+version, rendered require, rendered bind) whose Display produces the exact strings the wire always carried - pinned byte-exactly by a unit test, and proven against every golden by the contract suite passing without regeneration. The rejections table's kind/rule/version columns come from matching the variant; nothing parses display text. The invariant's version is recorded because the rejecting site is the only place that knows it and it is unbackfillable later.
- **One recording site.** Both public propose paths flow through `finalise_outcome`, which gained the pool for the post-rollback insert - so single runs, `--explain-on-reject`, batch rows, and refused compensations all record for free, each exactly once. The integration matrix pins the single, traced, rejection-state, and batch paths directly, and proves commits, kernel errors, and PG-layer errors write nothing; compensation recording is the same structural fact, not separately tested.
- **`constrained` is the strongest verdict, for any shape.** The headline case is the always-on prohibition: its enforcement work was invisible in committed history by construction, and it becomes measurable the moment the log shows it refusing - pinned end-to-end by a custody-shaped PG test. Attribution is two-sided: an invariant refusal counts for the named rule AND the proposing transformation; a gate refusal belongs to the transformation alone. Refusals naming rules or transformations today's programme does not declare surface flagged, the drift precedent extended.
- **The run envelope is unchanged.** `inspect rejections` mirrors `inspect audit` - typed rows, unpinned, outside the `result.json` contract; the coverage report's new refusal fields rode the triple-pin (schema, golden, Python models) in one stroke, the discipline's third designed exercise.

**Considered and rejected:** recording inside the refusing transaction (impossible by definition - the rollback is the point); a recording flag (refusals are the compliance story; an operational log nobody can silently disable is worth more than the knob); swallowing insert failures to preserve the rejected envelope (a broken database is operational truth the caller needs, and batch semantics stay honest through the existing error routing); parsing the reason string to recover the rule name for the durable record (the structured reason exists precisely so the table never depends on display text; the single-run stderr courtesy line still parses the backticked name from the envelope string, the diagnostics round's deliberate trade, because the wire envelope stays a plain string).

**The review round (both LLMs):** ChatGPT's one caveat was real and predated this PR: `collect_implications`' definition descent treated parameters as free from the caller's perspective, so a call-site-constrained define (`reviewed_at(3)`) could count `fired` from a claim the call never asked about (`RiskLevel(c, 2)`). The fix reuses the canonical machinery instead of growing a substitution engine: each collected implication now carries its `Defined` call chain, and coverage replays that chain through `definition_call_frame` - the same frames evaluation uses - before testing the antecedent, so literal and pre-bound arguments constrain the firing question exactly as they constrain enforcement. No variable-capture handling needed, because frames bind values rather than rewriting terms. The honest residual is recorded in the module doc: a call repeating an UNBOUND variable (`f(x, x)` at invariant scope) carries an equality constraint frames cannot express and can still overcount; regression tests pin the literal-constrained and nested-chain cases. ChatGPT's schema hardening also landed: a CHECK pins kind/version agreement (a versioned gate or an unversioned invariant cannot be inserted even by hand). Copilot's one comment - the per-rejection linear scan over invariant entries - became a name-to-index map built once at classification, the same fix the self-review had deferred until someone was forced to care; two independent reviewers counted as forced.

### The audit read contract: a projector leans, the tail gets blessed

**Forced by:** the exact moment the embedder contract had reserved language for. Glasshouse's projector - the read side of the ETRM - began tailing `morpholog.audit` directly, cursoring on `(committed_at, transition_id)` and folding claim arrays into projection tables; the deliberately-open bullet said the audit surface would earn its contract entry "when an embedder leans on it". Issue #136 offered two forms: pin the table, or bless a CLI surface. Both landed, with the CLI as THE contract: `inspect audit` streams NDJSON transitions, `--after <transition_id>` resumes, `--named <file.morph>` decodes the claim arrays, and the generated Python client grew `audit()`/`audit_named()` - the named decode for free, the issue's own argument for form two.

- **The headline is a refuted guarantee.** The first cursor-safety design ran each invocation under `SERIALIZABLE READ ONLY DEFERRABLE` and claimed lossless resume from SSI's safe snapshot. Pressure-testing against PostgreSQL's actual semantics killed it: `GetSafeSnapshot` waits for concurrent read-write transactions and then retroactively blesses the ORIGINAL snapshot when none conflicted, so an independent in-flight writer's row - invisible in the blessed snapshot, sorted below rows already emitted because `committed_at` is the writer's transaction START time - would be skipped by the cursor forever. Deferrable guarantees the serializability of one read; cursor resume spans two, and SSI says nothing across them. The replacement is a **start-time watermark**: compute the horizon (minimum `xact_start` over the database's open transactions, coalesced with `now()` in the SAME statement - a separate fallback query would reopen the window) BEFORE the snapshot, then page strictly below it. Rows at or above the horizon are withheld, never lost. The proof, its preconditions (stat visibility detected, never assumed; no 2PC), and the liveness caveat (the horizon trails the oldest open transaction) live in the rustdoc and the contract entry; the race test is the proof-in-code.
- **The recipe's order is baked into the API.** `begin_audit_tail` resolves the cursor, computes the horizon, then opens the `REPEATABLE READ READ ONLY` snapshot - a caller cannot misorder what the API sequences. The CLI is the recipe's stdout.
- **One opaque cursor token.** `--after` takes a transition id alone (every envelope, receipt, and audit line carries one); the issue's compound `<committed_at>,<transition_id>` form was rejected - two tokens invite hand-built timestamps, the exact ambiguity the contract closes. Unknown ids are an error naming the id, never a silent restart from zero.
- **NDJSON always, no flag.** The array shape was explicitly unpinned; the only blessed consumer is a tail; an array forces whole-log buffering against the constant-memory contract; `run --batch` receipts are the precedent.
- **`--named` decodes claims only.** `arguments` and `emitted_intents` stay tagged: transformation parameters and intent payloads are different vocabularies (parameter kinds, not predicate declarations), and decoding them would re-import the generator's refusal sweep into a read path. The asymmetry is stated in the contract, not papered over.
- **The table contract is one paragraph, not a second surface.** For direct SQL readers: the replay order is stable because it is load-bearing in verify, coverage, and as-of; the claim JSONB is the pinned `claim_instance`; columns grow additively; and `committed_at` is transaction-start time, so a direct tail needs the same horizon-first recipe - which the paragraph teaches, because the projector that forced this PR has the race today.
- **The flagged fetch-alls closed with the same machinery.** `reconstruct_inner` (verify, both as-of reads, derived-at) and verify's claims-table read now page in the shared replay chunk - the ops-hardening follow-up recorded on the coverage PR, landed where its pattern already lived.

**Considered and rejected:** the deferrable-snapshot guarantee (above - the review loop's job is exactly this); `--limit`/`--follow` flags (the poll loop is the projector's own); growing `inspect rejections` a matching `--after` (no leaner yet); decoding `arguments` by parameter name (a second decoder for a read path nothing needs); a configurable page size (no history has forced tuning).

### The CLI meets its first stranger: propose, check --ir, and help that earns its keep

**Forced by:** reading `morpholog` with no arguments as a newcomer would. The help was a wall of rustdoc (clap renders each variant's first doc paragraph in the command list, and ours were maintainer essays), and with the first external embedder regenerating its client from the binary, the last cheap moment for from-scratch naming was now.

**Landed:** every command's first doc line is one plain sentence (the depth moved below it, where `morpholog help <command>` still shows all of it); a header naming the version and what Morpholog is; a getting-started footer; journey ordering at both levels. And the two renames the redesign exercise surfaced: **`run` is now `propose`** - the kernel's own verb, because a change is a proposal the rules may lawfully refuse, and "run" suggested imperative execution of exactly the kind Morpholog exists to prevent - and **`parse` folded into `check --ir`**, a debugging view that had been wearing a top-level command's name, now deliberately behind validation so only a sound programme renders. The generated Python client follows (`run()` -> `propose()`, `run_batch()` -> `propose_batch()`, `submit()` unchanged as the typed sugar). **The rename stops at the wire:** the `run_outcome` `$defs` key and the Python `parse_run_outcome` keep their names - envelope identity is contract identity, and the verb was the wrong layer, not the wire.

**Considered and rejected:** a `run` deprecation alias (two names for one act is the drift the rename kills; the one embedder regenerates); renaming the wire's `run_outcome` def (a schema-identity break for zero semantic gain); renaming `submit` (it reads as what it is - submitting a proposal through a typed request).

### The unsupplied-antecedent lint: a declared-supplier smell, not a vacuity proof

**Forced by:** the same authoring smell the lint tier exists to catch - declaring a predicate, referencing it in an antecedent, and never giving it an admission path (a typo, or a transformation dropped in a later version). The shape is the sibling of an unused-declaration lint: a mismatch between the declared vocabulary and the programme's admission surface.

**What it is - and the boundary the review tightened.** The first draft framed this as proof that an antecedent can *never* bind. It cannot prove that, and saying so contradicts our own architecture. State is admitted **claims** accumulated over time; the audit log and claims table outlive any single source file; `inspect coverage` already reports transformations "present in history but absent from the current programme" as drift. So a predicate with no admitting transformation in the *current* programme is empty only **on a fresh ledger** - persisted, imported, or historically admitted claims can still populate it (drop a `settle` transformation in v2 and existing `Settled` claims remain). This is the same overclaim we refuted for the deferrable-snapshot guarantee: a multi-LLM review caught it before merge. The honest finding is an authoring signal, not a reachability proof - and it does **not** catch the motivating bitemporal-vacuity wound (a missing governing *claim* for an effective-time interval, where the predicate does have a supplier); that is a different, state-level question.

**Landed:** a second lint occupant, `UnsuppliedAntecedent`, hint-grade like the first, `--strict` promotes it, a source-located caret points at the invariant. The load-bearing decisions:

- **The honest mechanism is admitted-supplier satisfiability.** `declared_supplier_predicates` is what the *source file* can admit into the candidate state an invariant checks - transformation assertions only. Derived claims are excluded: they are read-side projections, never enumerated into candidate state, so a predicate produced only as a derived claim has no admitted supplier (and an invariant referencing one is rejected as undeclared before lints run anyway). `undeclared_blockers` walks an antecedent and returns the predicates with no such supplier that genuinely force it unsatisfiable on a fresh ledger - or `None` if it could bind. It evaluates the Boolean structure: an `and` is blocked by any blocked conjunct (only those are the cause); an `or` only when every branch is blocked (and then all branches are the cause, named collectively); negation, implication, `forall`, and value comparisons stay satisfiable. Returning the evidence from the *same* traversal that decides bindability is what stops the diagnostic naming an innocent predicate - an earlier shape that decided with one walk and reconstructed the reason with another would point at an `or` branch the live alternative bypassed, or at a value-position predicate the cut does not analyse.
- **One finding per invariant, blockers named together.** For an `or`, no single predicate is independently required, so the message names them as referenced "in an implication antecedent" rather than claiming each is mandatory. Findings come back in invariant-declaration order (one pass over invariants, each invariant's findings before the next; sub-expression ordering awaits the node-identified IR).
- **Only authored invariants are flagged.** A generated discipline invariant is machinery the author cannot see in source. The detector reuses the lint tier's `collect_implications` polarity walker, transitive through `define` bodies, so a requirement hidden behind a named condition is caught wherever spelled - and nested detection stays, with wording that says the invariant "contains an implication" whose antecedent is unsupplied, never that the whole invariant "enforces nothing" (a live implication beside a dead one still does its work).
- **The detector lives in `analysis`, not `lint`.** It is a general static analysis with direct unit tests over its Boolean algebra; the lint is one consumer.

**Considered and rejected:** framing it as a vacuity proof or a `coverage` verdict (the closed-world reachability it would need is not enforced and not worth building for a lint); a `DeadReason` evidence tree (a flat blocker set plus collective wording is honest enough); deciding bindability and reconstructing the reason in separate passes (false attribution); a constraint solver for contradiction-based vacuity, value-position deadness, and dead `require` gates (named residuals, sound and cheap to omit).

### The SQL views generator: a read contract, not a convenience dump

**Forced by:** the read side. The governed core admits state into `morpholog.claims` as a positional JSONB array per claim; every BI tool, and the external embedder's own projector, wants that state in plain typed SQL. Reading the JSONB by hand (`arguments -> 2 ->> 'value'`, positionally, against an undocumented shape) is the fragile thing the generated Python client already refused to let *writers* do. `morpholog generate views` is the read-side complement of `morpholog schema`: one typed view per declared **base** predicate over `morpholog.claims`, emitted as a script you pipe to `psql`.

**What it is.** A pure renderer (`morpholog generate views x.morph | psql`): no database, no async, same inputs → byte-identical bytes, so the drift discipline is regenerate-and-diff exactly as for the generated Python client (a committed golden, `examples/10_trade_lifecycle/trade_lifecycle_views.sql`, is diffed against fresh generation in CI). The load-bearing decisions, each what turns a dump into a contract:

- **Read-only by construction, not by convention.** Each view wraps its source in a top-level `WITH governed_claims AS (...)`. A simple single-table view in PostgreSQL is *automatically updatable* - `DELETE`/`UPDATE` through it would reach `morpholog.claims` and bypass the kernel entirely. The CTE disqualifies the view from auto-updatability, so writes through it fail. A live-DB test (`sql_views_pg.rs`) proves `INSERT`/`UPDATE`/`DELETE` all error and the base row is untouched. This is a read *interface*, not a security boundary - a role with direct write on `morpholog.claims` can still write; the docs say so.

- **Atomic application.** The whole script is wrapped `BEGIN; ... COMMIT;` (plain SQL, no `psql` metacommands), so a database-time failure - an incompatible existing view, an ownership error, a name already taken by a table - rolls back rather than leaving a half-built read surface. The CLI writes the script to stdout with `write_all`, never `println!`, so the piped bytes are byte-identical to the rendered script. A live test pre-creates a colliding table and confirms the rollback leaves no catalogue behind.

- **Metadata-first columns, plus the raw arguments.** Each view selects `_morpholog_asserted_in`, `_morpholog_asserted_at`, `_morpholog_arguments` *first*, then the business fields in declaration order. `CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW` permits only *appending* columns with existing names/order/types unchanged; metadata-first means appending a declared predicate field appends a view column - a compatible replace - whereas provenance-last would reject append-only model evolution. Two live tests pin this: appending a field is a compatible replace; renaming one is correctly rejected (the documented manual-migration case). The raw `_morpholog_arguments` also preserves the exact governed value behind every typed projection.

- **`_morpholog_` is a reserved prefix, not a name list.** A business field called `predicate_name` or `asserted_at` is lawful; only a `_morpholog_`-prefixed field is refused. Reserving the namespace rather than specific names keeps the collision rule stable as metadata columns are added.

- **A hash-pinned catalogue.** A `_morpholog_catalog` view (VALUES-backed, hence inherently non-updatable) carries the programme name, the canonical model hash - the *same* pin the generated Python client stamps - and the predicate→view inventory, giving SQL consumers a queryable model identity, a basis for drift-checking, and (deferred) a `--prune`. A zero-base programme gets a typed-empty catalogue, lawful rather than a refusal.

- **The kind→SQL map is exhaustive, with no `_` arm.** `column_sql` matches every `PredicateArgKind`; a new kind fails compilation rather than silently mis-projecting - the same discipline the python-client generator carries. Subject→`text`, Decimal→`numeric`, Date→`date`, Timestamp→`timestamptz`, Bool→`boolean`, Quantity→`numeric` over the amount (the unit recorded as `COMMENT ON COLUMN`). **`Collection` and `Any` are exposed as faithful `jsonb`** (`Any` as the whole tagged `{type,value}` object so consumers can discriminate) - diverging from the python-client floor, which *refuses* them, because a read projection can carry them where a typed write request cannot.

- **The precision boundary is documented and the negative-duration trap is handled.** PostgreSQL `timestamptz`/`interval` are microsecond-resolution; jiff timestamps/durations can carry nanoseconds, so the typed columns truncate below a microsecond while `_morpholog_arguments` keeps the exact source (a live test confirms a one-nanosecond pair collapses in the typed column but stays distinct in the raw column). The live test also surfaced a real correctness bug an offline design could not: jiff serialises a *negative* duration with a leading sign (`-PT6H`), which PostgreSQL's interval parser rejects - and because the cast lives in the view body, one negative-duration claim would break the whole view at read time. The Duration extractor therefore detects the leading `-`, strips it, and negates the parsed interval, so negative spans read correctly. This is exactly why the DB-behaviour tests exist alongside the text-pinning unit tests.

- **Identifier and literal safety.** View names are derived by an acronym-aware `snake_case` (`PPAContract` → `ppa_contract`, `TradeID` → `trade_id`); business field names are *preserved* (faithful to the declaration) and so must already be safe unquoted lowercase identifiers - refused otherwise, because PostgreSQL folds unquoted identifiers to lowercase and a mangled name is worse than a clear refusal. Over-63-byte names (silently truncated by PostgreSQL, hence invisibly collision-prone), SQL reserved words (so consumers needn't quote), and snake_case view-name collisions between two lawful predicate names are all refused. Refusal is whole-run - every finding collected before anything is written - the same discipline as the python-client sweep. Every identifier in the output is quoted anyway, and every string literal goes through one apostrophe-escaping helper.

- **Layering: the renderer lives in `morpholog-postgres`, not the kernel.** SQL over `morpholog.claims` - the JSONB shape, the PG types, the extractor operators - is PostgreSQL-substrate knowledge, and the crate already owns the claims↔JSONB wire mapping the extractors mirror (`EvalValue` in `state.rs` is the single source of truth). The CLI command is a thin wrapper.

**Scope - base predicates only.** Derived-claim heads are declared as predicates but computed by the kernel on demand, never materialized; they are skipped (filtered by `derived_claims`) and read via `inspect derived`. Materializing them means compiling a derived definition to SQL - the kernel→SQL residual-compilation spike the roadmap gates separately. Also deferred: materialized views/indexes, automatic `DROP`/`--prune` (the script never drops; the catalogue plus a `COMMENT ON VIEW` ownership marker let a consumer find stale views), grants/roles/RLS, and multi-dialect SQL.

**Considered and rejected:** simple auto-updatable views (they would silently expose a kernel-bypassing write path - the CTE is the whole point); a non-transactional script (a half-applied read surface on any mid-script failure); provenance columns last (breaks append-only `CREATE OR REPLACE` evolution); refusing `Any`/`Collection` like the python-client floor (faithful jsonb costs nothing on a read path); a typed `interval` column without the sign fix (one negative-duration row breaks the view); a shared Python/SQL/JSON-schema kind abstraction (three substrates with three different faithful mappings - premature); automatic `DROP ... CASCADE` of removed predicates' views (too destructive; surfaced via the catalogue instead).

### The derived read cache: the kernel computes, SQL projects

**Forced by:** the obvious next step after base-predicate views - "derived-claim views" - and the wall it hit. A derived claim's value is *computed by the kernel* (rust_decimal, nanosecond jiff). Recomputing it in SQL would force a false choice (PostgreSQL `numeric` division and microsecond `interval` cannot bit-match the kernel, so exactness vs coverage) and would commit the project to maintaining a second evaluator - a Prop/ValueExpr->SQL compiler - that must be differential-tested against the kernel forever. The reframe (reached over several review rounds): **SQL must not be a second evaluator.** The kernel already computes derived rows exactly via `enumerate_derived`; materialise *those* rows and let SQL project them, exactly as base views project `morpholog.claims`. The doctrine line, symmetric on both sides:

> SQL views are not the interpreter. They are a projection of interpreter-produced state. For base predicates that state is `morpholog.claims`; for derived predicates it is a refreshed read projection computed by `enumerate_derived`. SQL never evaluates Morpholog rules.

This is the roadmap's "materialised derived claims" realized as an operational read cache. It is also the deepest *subtraction* available here: by not building the compiler, the kernel stays the sole evaluator (the constitution: the interpreter is the spec), and there is no parallel SQL evaluator to keep honest.

**Landed:** `morpholog refresh derived <file.morph>` and `morpholog_read`, a read-model schema kept separate from governed `morpholog` (claims/audit/rejections/outbox) and from the `morpholog_views` BI surface - the three-schema story. The load-bearing decisions:

- **A read model, never governed state.** Nothing in `propose`, invariant evaluation, or value lookups reads `morpholog_read`; the cache cannot become evidence. A live test pins that a `propose` leaves the projection untouched (it is stale-by-design until the next refresh).
- **Exactness by construction.** Rows store the kernel's `ClaimInstance` arguments as tagged-JSONB, byte-shaped like `morpholog.claims` - so the exact computed value is preserved (a Timestamp-difference `Duration` round-trips through the cache and equals the kernel's nanosecond value; a live differential test confirms `cache == list_derived` for every derived claim). The future derived-view layer reuses the base-view extractors unchanged.
- **Freshness is operational metadata, loud - and honestly named.** Each generation records its model hash, refresh time, and the latest audit transition *visible in its read snapshot* - "as of the last refresh." Crucially this is a `source_snapshot_*` marker, NOT a `high-water`: `audit.committed_at` is the writer's transaction-start time while visibility follows commit order, so a transaction in flight at snapshot time (whose committed_at may sort *earlier* than the marker) is simply excluded and folded in by the next refresh. A review round caught the original "high-water / never overclaims" wording as the same overclaim the audit-tail watermark machinery exists to prevent; the cache is a snapshot, not an audit-resume coordinate, and the column names now say so (lossless resume is `inspect audit`'s job). The kernel's `inspect derived` stays the authoritative, always-live read (including `--as-of`). Modelling cache invalidation as ordinary claims (the roadmap's eventual preference for *governed* materialisation) is a deliberate non-goal here: this is a discardable read cache, not governed state.
- **Generation-based publish, not delete-and-replace.** A refresh builds a new generation under its own `refresh_id` and flips a single-row active pointer; readers stay on the prior generation until the flip commits. This decouples the heavy bulk insert from the instant swap and forward-proofs the persisted shape (the expensive thing to migrate later) for deferred optimisations.
- **Three phases keep the kernel compute outside any transaction.** A short `REPEATABLE READ` snapshot reads the latest visible audit transition and the scoped claims in one snapshot; the snapshot is released; the sync kernel computes; a short write transaction bulk-inserts the new generation (one `UNNEST` statement, not a round-trip per row), flips the pointer, and drops the old generation. Holding an MVCC snapshot open across a long Rust compute is the anti-pattern this avoids.

**Performance posture, stated honestly:** full refresh, single-threaded, exact. Cost scales with loaded claims + intermediate domain matches + emitted rows - good for operational stores, not yet a streaming analytical engine. Deferred by name (not snuck in): incremental / affected-key refresh, partitioned / per-predicate refresh, parallel refresh, COPY-binary bulk load, a streaming `enumerate_derived`, and `--max-*-claims` guard rails (the per-phase timing + count metrics in the summary give the visibility a v1 needs; guard flags wait for a real workload). The generation schema is what lets those land later without a data migration.

**Considered and rejected:** a derived-to-SQL compiler (a second evaluator with a permanent differential-test obligation - the whole point of the reframe is not to build it); refresh as part of `propose` (contaminates the commit path with read-model maintenance; freshness is operational, not semantic); `morpholog.derived_claims` beside governed tables (a read cache in the governed namespace reads as evidence; `morpholog_read` makes the distinction structural); delete-all/insert-all on the live table (write-amplifying churn and no reader isolation; generations give an instant atomic swap); `UNLOGGED` by default (faster but truncated on crash and not replicated - the canonical schema stays boring and safe; operators may opt in for a purely-local cache); TimescaleDB (a mutable replace-on-refresh snapshot is the wrong shape for a hypertable, and continuous aggregates can neither express the stateful-fold-then-compute derivation nor avoid reintroducing the second evaluator).

### Derived views: the read surface, completed

**Forced by:** the read cache existing but BI still having to read its tagged JSONB by hand - the same fragility base views removed for `morpholog.claims`. `morpholog generate views` now emits one unified read surface: base predicates as views over `morpholog.claims`, derived predicates as views over the `morpholog_read` cache, in the same atomic script.

**The de-risking finding: no kind inference.** A `derived X(...)` block is only valid if `predicate X(...)` is declared, and validation checks the head's `ArgDecl` kinds against the derived output (keys then values) position-by-position. So a derived view's typed columns come straight from the declared head predicate, through the *same* `column_sql` extractor base views use (the cache stores arguments in the same tagged-JSONB shape). The PR is a generalisation of the #145 renderer over a second source table, not a new analysis. The decisions:

- **The renderer is parameterised by source, not duplicated.** A `ViewKind` threads through `render_view`: base views wrap `morpholog.claims` in a `governed_claims` CTE with `_morpholog_asserted_in`/`_at` provenance; derived views wrap the active-generation join in a `governed_derived` CTE with `_morpholog_refreshed_at` / `_morpholog_model_hash` / `_morpholog_source_snapshot_{transition_id,committed_at}`. The non-updatable-CTE discipline, the metadata-first column order, the `column_sql` projection, and the identifier-refusal sweep are shared unchanged; the sweep and the snake_case collision check now span base and derived together (a base predicate and a derived head can collide on one view name).
- **A derived view filters on the generated model hash, not just the active generation.** `... AND r.model_hash = '<this surface's hash>'`. A cache refreshed for a *different* model carries rows whose shape may not match this view's columns, so the view shows them as zero rows rather than mis-projecting - the honest contract is "empty until `refresh derived` has run for this same `.morph`", covering no-refresh, different-model, and stale-older-model alike. A live test pins each case; another pins that the view follows the active-generation pointer after a new refresh, and that writes through it fail.
- **The catalogue gains `kind`, appended last.** `_morpholog_catalog` records `base | derived` per view; appending the column (not inserting it) keeps the catalogue itself a compatible `CREATE OR REPLACE` across versions. `RenderedViews` now reports base and derived counts, and the CLI prints the split.

This completes the read side: `generate views` for the shape, `refresh derived` to populate the derived half, and the kernel as the sole evaluator throughout. The plan-stage review (which flagged the model-hash filter as the must-fix) is recorded here because that filter is the difference between a correct read contract and a subtly-wrong one.

### The gate-protection map: which gate front-loads which invariant

**Forced by:** the verification arc's own gap. `inspect controls` already listed each transformation's gates and, separately, the invariant guarantees - the two questions an auditor asks side by side, but unconnected. The deeper tier the roadmap named was the cross-link: this `require` is the front line for that standing rule. It adds no IR primitive - a mechanical reading over the parsed programme, like every legibility surface.

**The relation, and the honesty boundary it is built around.** A gate `G` of transformation `T` **front-loads** an authored, implication-shaped invariant `A implies C` when `T` admits a predicate the antecedent `A` rests on (so the transformation can make the invariant relevant) and `G` positively references a predicate the consequent `C` also positively references. The signature reuses the lint tier's `positive_claims`, which already descends `define` bodies and tracks polarity (positive references across an `or`, weaker than "requires" - the wording is deliberate) - so the biometric case (gate and invariant both calling `two_distinct_prior_verifications`) and the inlined cases (KYC, trade) reduce to the same shared inner predicate with one mechanism, and `collect_implications` supplies the implications. Each `GateFrontLoad` names both sides: the `triggered_by` predicate `T` admits that puts the invariant in play, and the `shared` consequent predicate the gate also references. The whole computation lives in `controls.rs` plus `pub(crate)` visibility on `positive_claims` and a `predicates_asserted_by` helper.

The load-bearing decision is what the link is *allowed to claim*. A gate checks bound arguments in the pre-state; the invariant is the standing guarantee over the candidate state and is checked at commit regardless of any gate; other transformations exist. So the surface says the gate **front-loads** the invariant - a syntactic correspondence through shared positive predicates - never that it *proves* the invariant unbreakable. This is the same boundary the unsupplied-antecedent lint had to be walked back to: predicate-name overlap is not a proof of the same business condition, and a test pins that a gate and a consequent sharing a predicate with unrelated arguments still link, with the wording calling it a shared predicate, not an entailment. The field is named `front_loads`, not `protects`, for the same reason.

**Two findings made the surface honest by construction.** A `sum(..) <= qty` consequent (trade's quantity cap) has no positively-required predicate, because `positive_claims` treats a `sum` body as tolerating zero matches - so the settlement gate front-loads `settled_date_has_effective_terms` (the terms backstop) but is correctly **not** claimed to front-load the cap, which only the commit-time check enforces. And the per-link failure mode is rendered mechanically as `<antecedent> and not (<consequent>)` rather than read from `Guarantee::forbids` - a review caught that `forbids` is populated only for the `not(..)` shape, so it is empty for exactly the implication-shaped invariants this feature targets; the local rendering is always present and needs no broadening of the guarantees module.

**The review rounds (ChatGPT).** At plan stage the verdict approved the direction and tightened three things, all adopted - rename `protects` to `front_loads` (the field name overclaimed), do not lean on `Guarantee::forbids` for implication invariants (add the local `failure_shape`), and add the same-predicate-unrelated-arguments test that pins the no-overclaim wording. The post-implementation round carried three more, all adopted: rename the public type `GateProtection` to `GateFrontLoad` (the type name still said "protection"), soften "positively requires" to "positively references" through docs and output (honest about what the polarity walker proves, especially across an `or`), and surface the antecedent side of each link as `triggered_by` (the data was already computed - the intersection of the transformation's admitted predicates with the invariant's antecedent - it just was not shown), so the prose reads both sides: this transformation can make `IdentificationDecision` exist, and this gate checks `MatchVerified`, which is what the consequent also rests on. The invariant-side view (which invariants are backstops with no front-loading gate) was deferred as a distinct surface. The honest non-pairing of the quantity cap, which the review singled out as the kind of restraint the project should be proud of, falls out of the `sum`-body rule rather than being special-cased.

Copilot's one comment flagged that growing `GateControl` with a public field is semver-additive-breaking for downstream struct-literal construction. Moot today (0.0.1, no external consumers, output-only DTOs), but it reopened the project-wide `non_exhaustive` deferral for the narrow right case: the legibility surfaces (`controls` and `guarantees`) are output-only structs the runtime hands out and keeps growing - `GateControl` gained `consults`, then `front_loads`, and the deferred invariant-side view would grow it again. So `ControlMatrix`, `TransformationControls`, `GateControl`, `GateFrontLoad`, and `Guarantee` became `#[non_exhaustive]`: the kernel still builds them by literal, readers still read every field, and a future field is no longer a breaking change. The blanket `non_exhaustive` deferral stands for everything that is not an evolving output DTO.

### The invariant-side front-line coverage: the control matrix, completed

**Forced by:** the deferral above. The gate-protection map answered "what does this gate front-load?"; an auditor also needs the inverse - "where is the front line for this standing rule, and where is there none?". The data already existed (every gate's `front_loads` link), so this PR inverts it into `ControlMatrix::front_line_coverage` and renders it; no new analysis, no kernel primitive.

The load-bearing decisions all came from the plan-stage review (ChatGPT), and each guards against a quiet overclaim:
- **Implication-shape granularity, not per-invariant-name.** An invariant `(A implies B) and (C implies D)` with only the first front-loaded must not read as "covered". The relation is really gate-to-implication (each link carries a `failure_shape`), so the inverse keys on the failure shape: one row per implication, partial coverage stays visible.
- **Backstop vs dormant.** An empty `front_loaded_by` is two different findings: a *backstop* (a transformation can trigger the implication but no gate front-loads it - caught only at commit) or *dormant* (no declared transformation triggers it at all). Intersecting each implication's antecedent footprint with the transformations' admitted predicates tells them apart, so the surface does not make an untriggered rule sound as urgent as an unguarded one.
- **Honest domain, in the wording.** The relation only matches authored implication-shaped invariants (positive-predicate overlap, not entailment), so the heading says exactly that and the backstop line says "this implication shape", never "this invariant" - a prohibition or discipline invariant a gate happens to guard is simply outside the view, not mislabelled a gap. The inverse `GateRef` carries the same `form`/`triggered_by`/`shared` debug fields as the gate side, and the new `ControlMatrix` field is `#[serde(default, skip_serializing_if = "Vec::is_empty")]` so older JSON still deserialises and the field is omitted when empty. The inversion keys on `(invariant, failure_shape)`, not the shape alone, so two invariants that render the same implication shape keep separate front-loaders (a review catch).
