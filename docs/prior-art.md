# Morpholog: influences and prior art

This is the intellectual map behind the [roadmap](roadmap.md) directions - possibility pressure, not sequencing. Nothing here is planned or committed: the roadmap decides what gets built and when, and the doctrine in [`scope-and-ambition.md`](scope-and-ambition.md) decides what stays out. This file exists so that reasoning is not lost, not to license it.

Independent literature surveys across the roadmap directions keep converging - the same spine of provenance semirings, Cedar, SecPAL, DBSP, simplification theory, and the Rego anti-pattern recurs across them, which is real corroboration. The load-bearing pointers, with the calibrations that survived our own filter (a survey is a map, not gospel: one overstated a substrate swap, two said adopt-now for Catala where we hold awaiting-an-example, and the latest misread bitemporality as a runtime primitive and misattributed why we need inconsistency-tolerant checking - the corrected reason is recorded below):

- **Explanation engine.** The theoretical spine is *provenance semirings* (Green, Karvounarakis & Tannen, PODS 2007): why-provenance is the polynomial annotation of a derived result, and the "minimal missing evidence" / why-not answer is *Datalog abduction* over our Horn-clause invariants, which sits in NP and is often polynomial in practice. **Cedar** (AWS, 2023) is the closest production analogue - a deliberately decidable, schema-validated policy language with SMT-backed "why allowed / why denied" tooling - worth reading as design *validation*. Prefer deriving why-provenance on demand from the audit log we already keep over storing a provenance polynomial per claim (a stored column would bloat the append-only lake). The deepening path past today's one-hop checklist: *why-minimal provenance* (subset-minimal witnesses keep data complexity tractable) and SAT-based incremental enumeration of explanations, both bounded to the transition's read footprint. The "compile a dual negation query per gate, run it only on failure, inside the same transaction" pattern from the integrity-checking literature is the SQL-era shape of what `propose_against_pg_with_rejection_state` + `explain` already do in memory - same-snapshot diagnosis, never a second read.
- **Incremental integrity checking.** Nicolas (1982) *simplification* is the classical theory behind the whole compile-the-invariants direction: substitute the update's constants into the constraints and check only the residual case the write could violate, never the whole relation - predicate-scoped loading is the coarse version of this, and the kernel arc's SQL spike would be the precise one (the compiled WHERE clause IS the residual check). The load-bearing modern refinement is Decker & Martinenghi's *inconsistency-tolerant* (case-bound) checking: new writes provably introduce no fresh violations even when history holds legacy ones. Our calibration on the trigger: NOT bitemporal correction (corrections are supersessions here - admitted state never passes through an inconsistent phase the kernel can see), but **rule versioning and exception claims** - adopting a stricter invariant version on a live ledger must not demand repairing all history first, and exception claims are deliberate, audited cases where a base rule does not hold. Case-bound semantics is the spec requirement on the SQL spike, recorded in the roadmap's kernel arc.
- **Decidable core, verified offline.** Alloy 6 (Kodkod) and Apalache/TLA+ are the right *offline* specification oracles - model the claim state and transformations, bounded-check for impossible-or-wrongly-reachable states - not runtime components. Hard guardrail (reinforcing the no-host-functions floor): never embed an SMT or SAT solver *in the runtime*; bounded checkers are safe only over a fixed finite scope, and an in-runtime solver would let a user encode an undecidable problem and evaporate the decidable-core guarantee. Cedar's recipe for keeping that offline SMT fast and decidable - quantifier-free assertions, static typing, bounded set operations - is the discipline to copy.
- **Vacuity and rule coverage.** Hardware model checking solved this decades ago (Beer et al.; Kupferman & Vardi): a rule is vacuous when some subformula never affects its truth, and in a logic with polarity the test is cheap substitution - the lint tier's polarity walkers are already that machinery. The dynamic half is *mutation-replay coverage*: replay the audit log and ask, per invariant, whether its antecedent ever bound at all - zero bindings across the ledger's whole history means the rule has never constrained anything, whatever its text promises. That replay metric is the algorithm behind the verification arc's coverage item (`inspect coverage`); static antecedent-satisfiability belongs to the offline-oracle tier above.
- **Materialised derived claims.** The DBSP / Z-set algebra (Budiu et al. 2022; Feldera) is the right *design model* for maintaining derived-claim views incrementally over the append-only log - signed multiplicities give a principled basis for as-of and undo without recomputation. Borrow the algebra, not the engine. The in-substrate *mechanism* to evaluate first is `pg_ivm` (trigger-based incremental view maintenance inside PostgreSQL, transaction-respecting, non-recursive SQL only - which is what derived claims are today), before reaching for an external engine.
- **Obligations.** Monitor time-bounded obligations *a posteriori* over the append-only audit log, not inline: the FLAVOR three-valued reading (fulfilled / breached / **undefined** = deadline still in the future) fits our post-commit boundary and the outside-coordinator tick, and a-priori inline enforcement is brittle (it cannot accommodate emergency overrides). The algorithm family is MonPoly/VeriMon (metric first-order temporal logic; VeriMon is Isabelle-verified), and the semantics that fits our corrections model is Basin et al.'s *out-of-order* monitoring: a retroactive correction is processed as the **refinement of a knowledge gap**, and verdicts are monotone - once an obligation resolves true or false, late or corrected events can never invalidate the verdict. That is supersession's shape exactly. Model *contrary-to-duty* obligations - the secondary duty that activates when a primary deadline is missed - as first-class; avoid *standard* deontic logic as a foundation (its paradoxes make it unfit for a computational system).
- **Tamper evidence.** The hash-chained-audit item should be a Crosby-Wallach **Merkle history tree**, not a naive hash chain: it gives logarithmic *inclusion* proofs ("this transition is in the log") and *consistency* proofs ("today's log is an append-only extension of last quarter's"), which is what lets a regulator verify an exported evidence pack offline. Key rotation and revocation belong **inside the ledger** as first-class timestamped claims, with signature validity judged against the key-registry state at signing time - the external-interpretive-facts-become-claims doctrine (the tzdb argument) applied to PKI, against stateless out-of-band key management.
- **Positioning precedents.** The standing/authority/effective-rules triad has deep roots: Searle's constitutive rules and the Jones-Sergot *counts-as* formalisation (a gate is the counts-as operator - brute input becomes institutional fact in a named context), proof-carrying authorization (NAL, AURA's proof-logged "did-op" receipts - our audit rows are that idea without the dependent types), and archival diplomatics' split of *reliability* (the creator's controls at creation - our gates and invariants) from *authenticity* (the record untampered over time - `verify`, and the history tree above). The novelty claim that survives scrutiny is deliberately narrow: the components are decades old; Morpholog is a database-native, ACID, non-blockchain reification of them as one runtime. One pitfall worth carrying forward: delegation chains must stay acyclic and bounded (the "authority loop"), which extends the Zanzibar note below when higher-order authority lands.
- **SSI contention.** The Serial Safety Net and Aria literatures are design inspiration only - both presuppose owning the concurrency control, and the PostgreSQL substrate floor holds. Their shared move, static read/write-footprint extraction from a decidable rule language, independently confirms two things the bench already measured: value-level partitioning alone cannot relieve 40001 (the read footprint is the whole predicate until scoped loading narrows it), and argument-sensitive scoped loading is the real lever. Key-sliced single-writer routing (serialize same-footprint writes into one queue before they reach PostgreSQL) genuinely eliminates hot-key aborts but needs a resident process - a `morpholog serve`-era lever; until then it is embedder-side guidance.
- **Minimalism templates.** **SecPAL** (three deduction rules, decidable PTIME via constrained Datalog, auditable by non-programmers) is the textbook precedent for our discipline. **Catala**'s *default logic* (definition-under-conditions-with-exceptions) is the deepest match to how regulatory text is actually written - a strong candidate language affordance, but it awaits a worked example where explicit require-gates plus retraction genuinely break down. Not an adopt-now; surface expressiveness must be forced. The rules-as-code failure modes are the boundary conditions on this whole family: never automate open-textured terms ("reasonable efforts" stays a human judgment entering as an evidence claim under authority - example 13's shape), and never let a default-logic solver into the runtime (exceptions stay explicit, as standing and exception claims).

Tempting moves the surveys raised that we reject, because they cut against locked floors or arrive unforced:

- **XTDB v2 as a substrate.** Bitemporal, immutable log, PostgreSQL wire - but it does not provide our load-bearing guarantee, commit-time `SERIALIZABLE` invariant checking that makes bad state structurally uncommittable, and adopting it abandons the PostgreSQL-only correctness floor (its single-writer log is a different concurrency model from the SSI `propose_against_pg` relies on). Useful prior art for bitemporal mechanics, not a substrate candidate. Morpholog is not a database; the value is the legitimacy gate, not the time-travel storage. And the bitemporal mechanics are achievable in-substrate anyway - SQL:2011-style valid time via PostgreSQL range types (`tstzrange`) plus exclusion constraints - so there is nothing to swap out for.
- **OPA/Rego as a template.** The cautionary tale, not the cousin: it traded decidability and analyzability for expressiveness, has no tractable why-not, and is non-deterministic under optimisation. Morpholog is what Rego could have been had it stayed in the decidable fragment.
- **Zanzibar-style relationship graphs.** Recursive relationship-graph permission engines (Zanzibar, raw ReBAC) resolve access by traversing deep graphs at runtime, which breaks predictable latency and decidability unless the depth is rigidly bounded. Relevant the moment higher-order authority or delegation lands: compile any relationship query into bounded finite joins over the relational substrate, never an unbounded recursive traversal.
- **Witness networks for log roots.** Publishing the history tree's root hash somewhere external is a one-line operational practice; a blockchain or gossip-network witness ecosystem is infrastructure for an adversary model (a forking operator serving different regulators different logs) no worked example has presented. Unforced.
- **SQL-trigger custody of invariants.** Compiling residual checks to SQL is the kernel arc's direction, but enforcement authority stays with the kernel-as-executable-spec, differential-tested against the compiled form - checks get compiled, custody does not relocate into trigger definitions a DBA can alter.
- **The diplomatics persons taxonomy.** Classic diplomatics assigns every record distinct author/writer/originator/addressee/creator roles. Nothing forces that decomposition of our single actor today; it waits for an example where the distinction carries legal weight.
