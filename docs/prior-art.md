# Morpholog: influences and prior art

This is the intellectual map behind the [roadmap](roadmap.md) directions - possibility pressure, not sequencing. Nothing here is planned or committed: the roadmap decides what gets built and when, and the doctrine in [`scope-and-ambition.md`](scope-and-ambition.md) decides what stays out. This file exists so that reasoning is not lost, not to license it.

Two independent literature surveys across the roadmap directions converged strongly - the same spine of provenance semirings, Cedar, SecPAL, DBSP, and the Rego anti-pattern appears in both, which is real corroboration. The load-bearing pointers, with the calibrations that survived our own filter (a survey is a map, not gospel: one overstated a substrate swap, both said adopt-now for Catala where we hold awaiting-an-example):

- **Explanation engine.** The theoretical spine is *provenance semirings* (Green, Karvounarakis & Tannen, PODS 2007): why-provenance is the polynomial annotation of a derived result, and the "minimal missing evidence" / why-not answer is *Datalog abduction* over our Horn-clause invariants, which sits in NP and is often polynomial in practice. **Cedar** (AWS, 2023) is the closest production analogue - a deliberately decidable, schema-validated policy language with SMT-backed "why allowed / why denied" tooling - worth reading as design *validation*. Prefer deriving why-provenance on demand from the audit log we already keep over storing a provenance polynomial per claim (a stored column would bloat the append-only lake).
- **Decidable core, verified offline.** Alloy 6 (Kodkod) and Apalache/TLA+ are the right *offline* specification oracles - model the claim state and transformations, bounded-check for impossible-or-wrongly-reachable states - not runtime components. Hard guardrail (reinforcing the no-host-functions floor): never embed an SMT solver *in the runtime*; bounded checkers are safe only over a fixed finite scope, and an in-runtime solver would let a user encode an undecidable problem and evaporate the decidable-core guarantee. Cedar's recipe for keeping that offline SMT fast and decidable - quantifier-free assertions, static typing, bounded set operations - is the discipline to copy.
- **Materialised derived claims.** The DBSP / Z-set algebra (Budiu et al. 2022; Feldera) is the right *design model* for maintaining derived-claim views incrementally over the append-only log - signed multiplicities give a principled basis for as-of and undo without recomputation. Borrow the algebra, not the engine. The in-substrate *mechanism* to evaluate first is `pg_ivm` (trigger-based incremental view maintenance inside PostgreSQL, transaction-respecting, non-recursive SQL only - which is what derived claims are today), before reaching for an external engine.
- **Obligations.** Monitor time-bounded obligations *a posteriori* over the append-only audit log, not inline: the FLAVOR three-valued reading (fulfilled / breached / **undefined** = deadline still in the future) fits our post-commit boundary and the outside-coordinator tick, and a-priori inline enforcement is brittle (it cannot accommodate emergency overrides). Model *contrary-to-duty* obligations - the secondary duty or penalty that activates when a primary deadline is missed - as first-class, since "what must happen when the deadline IS missed" is a real requirement. Formalise with metric temporal logic / timed dyadic deontic logic; avoid *standard* deontic logic as a foundation - its paradoxes make it unfit for a computational system.
- **Minimalism templates.** **SecPAL** (three deduction rules, decidable PTIME via constrained Datalog, auditable by non-programmers) is the textbook precedent for our discipline. **Catala**'s *default logic* (definition-under-conditions-with-exceptions) is the deepest match to how regulatory text is actually written - a strong candidate language affordance, but it awaits a worked example where explicit require-gates plus retraction genuinely break down. Not an adopt-now; surface expressiveness must be forced.

Two tempting moves the survey raised that we reject, both because they cut against locked floors:

- **XTDB v2 as a substrate.** Bitemporal, immutable log, PostgreSQL wire - but it does not provide our load-bearing guarantee, commit-time `SERIALIZABLE` invariant checking that makes bad state structurally uncommittable, and adopting it abandons the PostgreSQL-only correctness floor (its single-writer log is a different concurrency model from the SSI `propose_against_pg` relies on). Useful prior art for bitemporal mechanics, not a substrate candidate. Morpholog is not a database; the value is the legitimacy gate, not the time-travel storage. And the bitemporal mechanics are achievable in-substrate anyway - SQL:2011-style valid time via PostgreSQL range types (`tstzrange`) plus exclusion constraints - so there is nothing to swap out for.
- **OPA/Rego as a template.** The cautionary tale, not the cousin: it traded decidability and analyzability for expressiveness, has no tractable why-not, and is non-deterministic under optimisation. Morpholog is what Rego could have been had it stayed in the decidable fragment.
- **Zanzibar-style relationship graphs.** Recursive relationship-graph permission engines (Zanzibar, raw ReBAC) resolve access by traversing deep graphs at runtime, which breaks predictable latency and decidability unless the depth is rigidly bounded. Relevant the moment higher-order authority or delegation lands: compile any relationship query into bounded finite joins over the relational substrate, never an unbounded recursive traversal.
