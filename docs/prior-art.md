# Morpholog: influences and prior art

This is the intellectual map behind the [roadmap](roadmap.md) directions - possibility pressure, not sequencing. Nothing here is planned or committed: the roadmap decides what gets built and when, and the doctrine in [`scope-and-ambition.md`](scope-and-ambition.md) decides what stays out. This file exists so that reasoning is not lost, not to license it.

Independent literature surveys across the roadmap directions keep converging - the same spine of provenance semirings, Cedar, SecPAL, DBSP, simplification theory, and the Rego anti-pattern recurs across them, which is real corroboration. Every entry below records the calibration that survived our own filter (a survey is a map, not gospel: one overstated a substrate swap, several said adopt-now for Catala where we hold awaiting-an-example, one misread bitemporality as a runtime primitive, one misattributed why we need inconsistency-tolerant checking, and one proposed conflict-resolution semantics for invariants that do not conflict).

- **Explanation engine.**
  - Keep: *provenance semirings* (Green, Karvounarakis & Tannen, PODS 2007) as the theoretical spine - why-provenance is the polynomial annotation of a derived result, and the why-not answer is *Datalog abduction* over our Horn-clause invariants (NP, often polynomial in practice). **Cedar** (AWS, 2023) as the closest production analogue and design validation: deliberately decidable, schema-validated, SMT-backed "why allowed / why denied".
  - The taxonomy that structures the tiers: *cause* (which present claim blocks - the deferred present-blockers tier), *missing evidence* (shipped), *repair plan* (the abduction tier). Missing-evidence lists order by actionability, never proof order (the Blawx lesson; ours already orders by candidate-supplier transformation).
  - The deepening path: *why-minimal provenance* (subset-minimal witnesses keep data complexity tractable), SAT-based incremental enumeration bounded to the transition's read footprint, and pattern-based provenance summaries (the PUG line, built for our non-recursive-with-negation fragment) when a checklist grows long. The repair-tractability boundary is mapped: semi-positive unions of conjunctive queries admit polynomial exact repair enumeration; projection-plus-join is set-cover-hard - the invariant fragment determines where exact repair ends and templates begin.
  - Calibration: derive why-provenance on demand from the audit log we already keep; never store a provenance polynomial per claim (it would bloat the append-only lake). The "dual negation query per gate, run only on failure, same transaction" pattern is the SQL-era shape of what `propose_against_pg_with_rejection_state` + `explain` already do in memory.
- **Incremental integrity checking.**
  - Keep: Nicolas (1982) *simplification* - substitute the update's constants into the constraints and check only the residual case the write could violate. Predicate-scoped loading is the coarse version; the kernel arc's SQL spike is the precise one (the compiled WHERE clause IS the residual check). Decker & Martinenghi's *inconsistency-tolerant* (case-bound) checking is the spike's spec requirement: new writes provably introduce no fresh violations even when history holds legacy ones.
  - Calibration on the trigger: NOT bitemporal correction (corrections are supersessions here - admitted state never passes through an inconsistent phase the kernel can see), but **rule versioning and exception claims** - adopting a stricter invariant version on a live ledger must not demand repairing all history first, and exception claims are deliberate, audited cases where a base rule does not hold.
  - The deepest connection: *ideal* simplification (residual checks both necessary and sufficient) exists exactly when query containment for the constraint language is decidable - the decidable-core floor is not just a safety property, it is what buys exact compilation. And the legal-theory side supplies the rule-change semantics by name (Governatori-Rotolo): *abrogation* governs the future while decisions under the old rule remain valid records - already our doctrine, on a two-timeline model - where *annulment* reaches back; standard belief revision is provably inadequate for sequential norm change.
  - Engineering constraints on the spike: compile from a small invariant normal form (denial constraints plus guarded existentials), orient delta-first (a `Transition` already IS the explicit delta algebra the transition-rules literature asks for), and give every compiled invariant an `EXPLAIN`/predicate-footprint regression test - compiled rules are query-planning-sensitive.
  - Not now: general incremental Datalog machinery (DRed, differential dataflow) - for the non-recursive invariant fragment, classical simplification beats it outright; the differential algebra stays on the derived-claims side.
- **Decidable core, verified offline.** Alloy 6 (Kodkod) and Apalache/TLA+ are the right *offline* specification oracles - model the claim state and transformations, bounded-check for impossible-or-wrongly-reachable states - not runtime components. Hard guardrail (reinforcing the no-host-functions floor): never embed an SMT or SAT solver *in the runtime*; bounded checkers are safe only over a fixed finite scope, and an in-runtime solver would let a user encode an undecidable problem and evaporate the decidable-core guarantee. Cedar's recipe for keeping that offline SMT fast and decidable - quantifier-free assertions, static typing, bounded set operations - is the discipline to copy.
- **Vacuity and rule coverage.**
  - Keep: hardware model checking solved this decades ago (Beer et al.; Kupferman & Vardi) - a rule is vacuous when some subformula never affects its truth, and in a logic with polarity the test is cheap substitution. The lint tier's polarity walkers are already that machinery.
  - The dynamic half is *mutation-replay coverage*: replay the audit log and ask, per invariant, whether its antecedent ever bound - zero bindings across the ledger's whole history means the rule has never constrained anything, whatever its text promises. The coverage-metrics literature supplies the report vocabulary: *fired*, *constrained*, *vacuous*, *dead antecedent* - the shape mutation testing of specifications independently arrives at (a mutant that survives full-history replay is a rule doing no work).
  - Sequencing: the replay metric is the algorithm behind `inspect coverage`; static antecedent-satisfiability belongs to the offline-oracle tier above.
- **Materialised derived claims.** The DBSP / Z-set algebra (Budiu et al. 2022; Feldera) is the right *design model* for maintaining derived-claim views incrementally over the append-only log - signed multiplicities give a principled basis for as-of and undo without recomputation. Borrow the algebra, not the engine. The in-substrate *mechanism* to evaluate first is `pg_ivm` (trigger-based incremental view maintenance inside PostgreSQL, transaction-respecting, non-recursive SQL only - which is what derived claims are today), before reaching for an external engine.
- **Obligations.**
  - Keep: monitor time-bounded obligations *a posteriori* over the append-only audit log, never inline (the FLAVOR three-valued reading - fulfilled / breached / **undefined** = deadline still in the future - fits the post-commit boundary and the outside-coordinator tick; a-priori inline enforcement cannot accommodate emergency overrides). The algorithm family is MonPoly/VeriMon (metric first-order temporal logic; VeriMon is Isabelle-verified).
  - The semantics that fits our corrections model is Basin et al.'s *out-of-order* monitoring: a retroactive correction is processed as the **refinement of a knowledge gap**, and verdicts are monotone - once an obligation resolves true or false, late or corrected events can never invalidate the verdict. That is supersession's shape exactly.
  - Boundaries: bounded-future safety fragment first, past-time preferred (no unbounded liveness promises in a verdict an auditor relies on); corrections reopen monitoring windows only within bounded horizons - a watermark, so verdict finality is itself a stated policy. The stream-runtime-verification shape (verdicts as derived output streams over the log) rhymes with derived claims. Model *contrary-to-duty* obligations - the secondary duty when a deadline is missed - as first-class; avoid *standard* deontic logic as a foundation (its paradoxes make it unfit for a computational system).
- **Tamper evidence.**
  - Keep: a Crosby-Wallach **Merkle history tree**, not a naive hash chain - logarithmic *inclusion* proofs ("this transition is in the log") and *consistency* proofs ("today's log is an append-only extension of last quarter's") are what let a regulator verify an exported evidence pack offline. Speak the Certificate Transparency proof vocabulary (checkpoint, tree size, inclusion proof, consistency proof, signed tree head) rather than inventing one.
  - The trap, named precisely: inclusion and consistency cannot prove **subject completeness** - that a pack contains *all* of one subject's history, nothing omitted. That needs a verifiable subject-indexed map (CONIKS-style per-subject roots committed under the global root) or an honestly narrower pack claim ("everything here is genuine and the log only grows").
  - Constraints: transparency protocols are subtle enough that the verification protocol should be formally modelled offline before any pack is called regulator-grade (the trees are the easy part; fork detection and verifier assumptions are not). Key rotation and revocation belong **inside the ledger** as first-class timestamped claims, with signature validity judged against the key-registry state at signing time - the external-interpretive-facts-become-claims doctrine (the tzdb argument) applied to PKI.
- **Positioning precedents.**
  - The theory: Searle's constitutive rules and the Jones-Sergot *counts-as* formalisation (a gate is the counts-as operator - brute input becomes institutional fact in a named context); proof-carrying authorization (NAL, AURA's proof-logged "did-op" receipts - our audit rows are that idea without the dependent types); archival diplomatics' split of *reliability* (the creator's controls at creation - our gates and invariants) from *authenticity* (the record untampered over time - `verify`, and the history tree above).
  - The closest *operational* predecessor: the electronic-institutions line (ISLANDER/AMELI) - runtime refusal of institutionally invalid actions existed there, without authority delegation or revocation, without an audit log sufficient for reconstruction, and without self-explaining refusals.
  - The claim that survives scrutiny: **novel runtime synthesis, not novel theory** - the components are decades old, and no surveyed prior OLTP runtime makes "an admitted claim with decision-specific standing, granted and revoked by named authorities, under the rules in force at the time, superseded rather than erased" its core admission primitive.
  - Carried forward: delegation chains must stay acyclic and bounded (the "authority loop"), extending the Zanzibar note below when higher-order authority lands.
- **Corrections and known-at semantics.**
  - The sharp question our supersession model answers: "the terms in force on day D, as known at time T, under rule bundle R" - three coordinates, the third already answered per transition by the audit row plus the programme hash, not by a kernel axis.
  - Warnings: snapshot aggregation over interval-stamped corrected records is subtly wrong in standard SQL semantics (the K-relations work names the bugs - relevant the day materialised derived claims meet corrections); "latest revised truth" versus "what was known then" are different datasets (the economics vintage-data lesson; *restatement* and *vintage* are also the vocabulary finance users already trust).
  - The characterisation test to pin when effective-rules modelling deepens (Snodgrass): a rule in force over valid time T1-T2 but only recorded at T3 must NOT be returned by "in force at T1.5 as known at T2.5" when T2.5 precedes T3 - the edge naive implementations get wrong. The accounting literature's classification of *why* a record changed - error correction, late evidence, changed estimate, amended rule - is modelling vocabulary for a future correction-heavy worked example, as predicates, not kernel sorts.
- **SSI contention.**
  - Keep as inspiration only: the Serial Safety Net and Aria literatures presuppose owning the concurrency control; the PostgreSQL substrate floor holds. Their shared move - static read/write-footprint extraction from a decidable rule language - independently confirms two things the bench already measured: value-level partitioning alone cannot relieve 40001 (the read footprint is the whole predicate until scoped loading narrows it), and argument-sensitive scoped loading is the real lever.
  - Levers: key-sliced single-writer routing (serialize same-footprint writes into one queue before they reach PostgreSQL) genuinely eliminates hot-key aborts but needs a resident process - a `morpholog serve`-era lever; until then it is embedder-side guidance. One cheap pointer from the PostgreSQL SSI papers themselves: `SERIALIZABLE READ ONLY DEFERRABLE` gives replay / export / verification reads a guaranteed-serializable snapshot with zero SSI footprint - worth taking whenever those read paths are next touched.
- **Minimalism templates.**
  - Keep: **SecPAL** (three deduction rules, decidable PTIME via constrained Datalog, auditable by non-programmers) as the textbook precedent for our discipline. **Catala**'s *default logic* (definition-under-conditions-with-exceptions) is the deepest match to how regulatory text is actually written - a strong candidate language affordance, but it awaits a worked example where explicit require-gates plus retraction genuinely break down. Not an adopt-now; surface expressiveness must be forced.
  - The boundary conditions, from the rules-as-code failure modes: never automate open-textured terms ("reasonable efforts" stays a human judgment entering as an evidence claim under authority - example 13's shape); never let a default-logic solver into the runtime (exceptions stay explicit, as standing and exception claims).
  - The survivor's discipline is co-location: rules carry citations to the clauses they implement (example 13's statute map already does this; Catala makes it the language's structure), and amendments are new rule versions with their own validity, never overwrites - the lesson running unbroken from the British Nationality Act formalisation onward.

Tempting moves the surveys raised that we reject, because they cut against locked floors or arrive unforced:

- **XTDB v2 as a substrate.** Bitemporal, immutable log, PostgreSQL wire - but it does not provide our load-bearing guarantee, commit-time `SERIALIZABLE` invariant checking that makes bad state structurally uncommittable, and adopting it abandons the PostgreSQL-only correctness floor (its single-writer log is a different concurrency model from the SSI `propose_against_pg` relies on). Useful prior art for bitemporal mechanics, not a substrate candidate. Morpholog is not a database; the value is the legitimacy gate, not the time-travel storage. And the bitemporal mechanics are achievable in-substrate anyway - SQL:2011-style valid time via PostgreSQL range types (`tstzrange`) plus exclusion constraints - so there is nothing to swap out for.
- **OPA/Rego as a template.** The cautionary tale, not the cousin: it traded decidability and analyzability for expressiveness, has no tractable why-not, and is non-deterministic under optimisation. Morpholog is what Rego could have been had it stayed in the decidable fragment.
- **Zanzibar-style relationship graphs.** Recursive relationship-graph permission engines (Zanzibar, raw ReBAC) resolve access by traversing deep graphs at runtime, which breaks predictable latency and decidability unless the depth is rigidly bounded. Relevant the moment higher-order authority or delegation lands: compile any relationship query into bounded finite joins over the relational substrate, never an unbounded recursive traversal.
- **Witness networks for log roots.** Publishing the history tree's root hash somewhere external is a one-line operational practice; a blockchain or gossip-network witness ecosystem is infrastructure for an adversary model (a forking operator serving different regulators different logs) no worked example has presented. Unforced.
- **SQL-trigger custody of invariants.** Compiling residual checks to SQL is the kernel arc's direction, but enforcement authority stays with the kernel-as-executable-spec, differential-tested against the compiled form - checks get compiled, custody does not relocate into trigger definitions a DBA can alter.
- **The diplomatics persons taxonomy.** Classic diplomatics assigns every record distinct author/writer/originator/addressee/creator roles. Nothing forces that decomposition of our single actor today; it waits for an example where the distinction carries legal weight.
- **Hohfeldian categories as kernel sorts.** The normative-positions literature suggests separating standing, power, permission, and immunity in the model. They ARE separate in Morpholog - as the modeller's predicates (`MayConfirm` is a permission claim; a grant transformation is a power exercise), which is the counts-as reading working as intended. Kernel-level normative sorts would be type-over-claims drift, awaiting an example where the predicate vocabulary genuinely cannot express the distinction.
- **Proof-tree objects per claim.** Authorization logics suggest persisting a says/speaks-for proof object with every decision. The audit row plus trace already is the proof object - actor, gates evaluated, invariants checked, programme hash - in deterministic template-renderable form; formal proof-term machinery is unforced until delegation chains exist for it to prove something about (and richer actor lineage is already a named deferred item).
- **A business-vocabulary (SBVR-style) glossary layer.** A standardised term layer above the rules is a commercialisation concern, not a runtime one; the declared predicate vocabulary plus learner's-guide `.morph` comments carry the meaning today. Unforced.
- **Argumentation-framework conflict resolution (Dung grounded semantics, priority relations).** Proposed as the semantics for "when two invariants conflict". Morpholog invariants do not compete - they are conjunctive, all must hold - so a genuine contradiction is a modelling bug whose right home is the verification arc's offline mutual-satisfiability check, and exceptions are explicit typed claims, leaving nothing for a runtime priority relation to adjudicate. Defeasibility stays in the Catala awaiting-an-example column above.
