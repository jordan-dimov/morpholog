# Morpholog: influences and prior art

This is the intellectual map behind the [roadmap](roadmap.md) directions - possibility pressure, not sequencing. Nothing here is planned or committed: the roadmap decides what gets built and when, and the doctrine in [`scope-and-ambition.md`](scope-and-ambition.md) decides what stays out. This file exists so that reasoning is not lost, not to license it.

Independent literature surveys across the roadmap directions keep converging - the same spine of provenance semirings, Cedar, SecPAL, DBSP, simplification theory, and the Rego anti-pattern recurs across them, which is real corroboration. The load-bearing pointers, with the calibrations that survived our own filter (a survey is a map, not gospel: one overstated a substrate swap, two said adopt-now for Catala where we hold awaiting-an-example, and the latest misread bitemporality as a runtime primitive and misattributed why we need inconsistency-tolerant checking - the corrected reason is recorded below):

- **Explanation engine.** The theoretical spine is *provenance semirings* (Green, Karvounarakis & Tannen, PODS 2007): why-provenance is the polynomial annotation of a derived result, and the "minimal missing evidence" / why-not answer is *Datalog abduction* over our Horn-clause invariants, which sits in NP and is often polynomial in practice. **Cedar** (AWS, 2023) is the closest production analogue - a deliberately decidable, schema-validated policy language with SMT-backed "why allowed / why denied" tooling - worth reading as design *validation*. Prefer deriving why-provenance on demand from the audit log we already keep over storing a provenance polynomial per claim (a stored column would bloat the append-only lake). The deepening path past today's one-hop checklist: *why-minimal provenance* (subset-minimal witnesses keep data complexity tractable) and SAT-based incremental enumeration of explanations, both bounded to the transition's read footprint. The "compile a dual negation query per gate, run it only on failure, inside the same transaction" pattern from the integrity-checking literature is the SQL-era shape of what `propose_against_pg_with_rejection_state` + `explain` already do in memory - same-snapshot diagnosis, never a second read. The why-not literature's three-level taxonomy structures the tiers: *cause* (which present claim blocks - the deferred present-blockers tier), *missing evidence* (shipped), *repair plan* (the abduction tier) - with missing-evidence lists ordered by actionability, never proof order (the Blawx lesson; ours already orders by candidate-supplier transformation), and repair staying bounded and template-driven: recent complexity results on missing-answer repairs confirm general repair planning goes intractable fast.
- **Incremental integrity checking.** Nicolas (1982) *simplification* is the classical theory behind the whole compile-the-invariants direction: substitute the update's constants into the constraints and check only the residual case the write could violate, never the whole relation - predicate-scoped loading is the coarse version of this, and the kernel arc's SQL spike would be the precise one (the compiled WHERE clause IS the residual check). The load-bearing modern refinement is Decker & Martinenghi's *inconsistency-tolerant* (case-bound) checking: new writes provably introduce no fresh violations even when history holds legacy ones. Our calibration on the trigger: NOT bitemporal correction (corrections are supersessions here - admitted state never passes through an inconsistent phase the kernel can see), but **rule versioning and exception claims** - adopting a stricter invariant version on a live ledger must not demand repairing all history first, and exception claims are deliberate, audited cases where a base rule does not hold. Case-bound semantics is the spec requirement on the SQL spike, recorded in the roadmap's kernel arc. Three engineering disciplines from the same literature bind the spike's shape: compile from a small invariant *normal form* (denial constraints plus guarded existentials - Christiansen & Martinenghi's transformation framework wants a normal form, not arbitrary ASTs); orient *delta-first* (Kowalski-Sadri: ask "which invariants can this delta falsify", which is the direction the read/write-set walkers already point, and a `Transition` already IS the explicit delta algebra Olive's transition rules ask for); and give every compiled invariant an `EXPLAIN`/predicate-footprint regression test, because once rules compile to SQL their cost and SSI footprint become query-planning-sensitive.
- **Decidable core, verified offline.** Alloy 6 (Kodkod) and Apalache/TLA+ are the right *offline* specification oracles - model the claim state and transformations, bounded-check for impossible-or-wrongly-reachable states - not runtime components. Hard guardrail (reinforcing the no-host-functions floor): never embed an SMT or SAT solver *in the runtime*; bounded checkers are safe only over a fixed finite scope, and an in-runtime solver would let a user encode an undecidable problem and evaporate the decidable-core guarantee. Cedar's recipe for keeping that offline SMT fast and decidable - quantifier-free assertions, static typing, bounded set operations - is the discipline to copy.
- **Vacuity and rule coverage.** Hardware model checking solved this decades ago (Beer et al.; Kupferman & Vardi): a rule is vacuous when some subformula never affects its truth, and in a logic with polarity the test is cheap substitution - the lint tier's polarity walkers are already that machinery. The dynamic half is *mutation-replay coverage*: replay the audit log and ask, per invariant, whether its antecedent ever bound at all - zero bindings across the ledger's whole history means the rule has never constrained anything, whatever its text promises. That replay metric is the algorithm behind the verification arc's coverage item (`inspect coverage`); static antecedent-satisfiability belongs to the offline-oracle tier above. The coverage-metrics literature supplies the report vocabulary: per rule, *fired* (antecedent bound), *constrained* (changed an outcome), *vacuous* (held without ever binding), *dead antecedent* (cannot bind) - a richer verdict than a binary "ever bound", and the shape mutation testing of specifications independently arrives at (a mutant that survives full-history replay is a rule doing no work).
- **Materialised derived claims.** The DBSP / Z-set algebra (Budiu et al. 2022; Feldera) is the right *design model* for maintaining derived-claim views incrementally over the append-only log - signed multiplicities give a principled basis for as-of and undo without recomputation. Borrow the algebra, not the engine. The in-substrate *mechanism* to evaluate first is `pg_ivm` (trigger-based incremental view maintenance inside PostgreSQL, transaction-respecting, non-recursive SQL only - which is what derived claims are today), before reaching for an external engine.
- **Obligations.** Monitor time-bounded obligations *a posteriori* over the append-only audit log, not inline: the FLAVOR three-valued reading (fulfilled / breached / **undefined** = deadline still in the future) fits our post-commit boundary and the outside-coordinator tick, and a-priori inline enforcement is brittle (it cannot accommodate emergency overrides). The algorithm family is MonPoly/VeriMon (metric first-order temporal logic; VeriMon is Isabelle-verified), and the semantics that fits our corrections model is Basin et al.'s *out-of-order* monitoring: a retroactive correction is processed as the **refinement of a knowledge gap**, and verdicts are monotone - once an obligation resolves true or false, late or corrected events can never invalidate the verdict. That is supersession's shape exactly. Model *contrary-to-duty* obligations - the secondary duty that activates when a primary deadline is missed - as first-class; avoid *standard* deontic logic as a foundation (its paradoxes make it unfit for a computational system). Two boundaries from the monitoring literature: start with the bounded-future safety fragment, past-time preferred (no unbounded liveness promises in a verdict an auditor relies on), and let corrections reopen monitoring windows only within bounded horizons - a watermark, so verdict finality is itself a stated policy. The stream-runtime-verification shape (verdicts as derived output streams over the log, not imperative jobs) rhymes with derived claims.
- **Tamper evidence.** The hash-chained-audit item should be a Crosby-Wallach **Merkle history tree**, not a naive hash chain: it gives logarithmic *inclusion* proofs ("this transition is in the log") and *consistency* proofs ("today's log is an append-only extension of last quarter's"), which is what lets a regulator verify an exported evidence pack offline - speak the Certificate Transparency proof vocabulary (checkpoint, tree size, inclusion proof, consistency proof, signed tree head) rather than inventing one. The trap the literature names precisely: inclusion and consistency cannot prove **subject completeness** - that a pack contains *all* of one subject's history, nothing omitted. That needs a verifiable subject-indexed map (CONIKS-style per-subject roots committed under the global root) or an honestly narrower pack claim ("everything here is genuine and the log only grows"). And transparency protocols are subtle enough that the verification protocol should be formally modelled offline before any pack is called regulator-grade - the trees are the easy part; fork detection and verifier assumptions are not. Key rotation and revocation belong **inside the ledger** as first-class timestamped claims, with signature validity judged against the key-registry state at signing time - the external-interpretive-facts-become-claims doctrine (the tzdb argument) applied to PKI, against stateless out-of-band key management.
- **Positioning precedents.** The standing/authority/effective-rules triad has deep roots: Searle's constitutive rules and the Jones-Sergot *counts-as* formalisation (a gate is the counts-as operator - brute input becomes institutional fact in a named context), proof-carrying authorization (NAL, AURA's proof-logged "did-op" receipts - our audit rows are that idea without the dependent types), and archival diplomatics' split of *reliability* (the creator's controls at creation - our gates and invariants) from *authenticity* (the record untampered over time - `verify`, and the history tree above). The novelty claim that survives scrutiny: **novel runtime synthesis, not novel theory** - the components are decades old, and no surveyed prior OLTP runtime makes "an admitted claim with decision-specific standing, granted and revoked by named authorities, under the rules in force at the time, superseded rather than erased" its core admission primitive. One pitfall worth carrying forward: delegation chains must stay acyclic and bounded (the "authority loop"), which extends the Zanzibar note below when higher-order authority lands.
- **SSI contention.** The Serial Safety Net and Aria literatures are design inspiration only - both presuppose owning the concurrency control, and the PostgreSQL substrate floor holds. Their shared move, static read/write-footprint extraction from a decidable rule language, independently confirms two things the bench already measured: value-level partitioning alone cannot relieve 40001 (the read footprint is the whole predicate until scoped loading narrows it), and argument-sensitive scoped loading is the real lever. Key-sliced single-writer routing (serialize same-footprint writes into one queue before they reach PostgreSQL) genuinely eliminates hot-key aborts but needs a resident process - a `morpholog serve`-era lever; until then it is embedder-side guidance. One cheap pointer from the PostgreSQL SSI papers themselves: `SERIALIZABLE READ ONLY DEFERRABLE` gives replay / export / verification reads a guaranteed-serializable snapshot with zero SSI footprint - worth taking whenever those read paths are next touched.
- **Corrections and known-at semantics.** The temporal-database and real-time-data literatures sharpen the question our supersession model answers: "the terms in force on day D, as known at time T, under rule bundle R" - three coordinates, and the third is already answered per transition by the audit row plus the programme hash, not by a kernel axis. Two transferable warnings: snapshot aggregation over interval-stamped corrected records is subtly wrong in standard SQL semantics (the K-relations work names the bugs - relevant the day materialised derived claims meet corrections), and "latest revised truth" versus "what was known then" are different datasets (the economics vintage-data lesson; *restatement* and *vintage* are also the vocabulary finance users already trust). The accounting literature's classification of *why* a record changed - error correction, late evidence, changed estimate, amended rule - is modelling vocabulary for a future correction-heavy worked example, as predicates, not kernel sorts.
- **Minimalism templates.** **SecPAL** (three deduction rules, decidable PTIME via constrained Datalog, auditable by non-programmers) is the textbook precedent for our discipline. **Catala**'s *default logic* (definition-under-conditions-with-exceptions) is the deepest match to how regulatory text is actually written - a strong candidate language affordance, but it awaits a worked example where explicit require-gates plus retraction genuinely break down. Not an adopt-now; surface expressiveness must be forced. The rules-as-code failure modes are the boundary conditions on this whole family: never automate open-textured terms ("reasonable efforts" stays a human judgment entering as an evidence claim under authority - example 13's shape), and never let a default-logic solver into the runtime (exceptions stay explicit, as standing and exception claims).

Tempting moves the surveys raised that we reject, because they cut against locked floors or arrive unforced:

- **XTDB v2 as a substrate.** Bitemporal, immutable log, PostgreSQL wire - but it does not provide our load-bearing guarantee, commit-time `SERIALIZABLE` invariant checking that makes bad state structurally uncommittable, and adopting it abandons the PostgreSQL-only correctness floor (its single-writer log is a different concurrency model from the SSI `propose_against_pg` relies on). Useful prior art for bitemporal mechanics, not a substrate candidate. Morpholog is not a database; the value is the legitimacy gate, not the time-travel storage. And the bitemporal mechanics are achievable in-substrate anyway - SQL:2011-style valid time via PostgreSQL range types (`tstzrange`) plus exclusion constraints - so there is nothing to swap out for.
- **OPA/Rego as a template.** The cautionary tale, not the cousin: it traded decidability and analyzability for expressiveness, has no tractable why-not, and is non-deterministic under optimisation. Morpholog is what Rego could have been had it stayed in the decidable fragment.
- **Zanzibar-style relationship graphs.** Recursive relationship-graph permission engines (Zanzibar, raw ReBAC) resolve access by traversing deep graphs at runtime, which breaks predictable latency and decidability unless the depth is rigidly bounded. Relevant the moment higher-order authority or delegation lands: compile any relationship query into bounded finite joins over the relational substrate, never an unbounded recursive traversal.
- **Witness networks for log roots.** Publishing the history tree's root hash somewhere external is a one-line operational practice; a blockchain or gossip-network witness ecosystem is infrastructure for an adversary model (a forking operator serving different regulators different logs) no worked example has presented. Unforced.
- **SQL-trigger custody of invariants.** Compiling residual checks to SQL is the kernel arc's direction, but enforcement authority stays with the kernel-as-executable-spec, differential-tested against the compiled form - checks get compiled, custody does not relocate into trigger definitions a DBA can alter.
- **The diplomatics persons taxonomy.** Classic diplomatics assigns every record distinct author/writer/originator/addressee/creator roles. Nothing forces that decomposition of our single actor today; it waits for an example where the distinction carries legal weight.
- **Hohfeldian categories as kernel sorts.** The normative-positions literature suggests separating standing, power, permission, and immunity in the model. They ARE separate in Morpholog - as the modeller's predicates (`MayConfirm` is a permission claim; a grant transformation is a power exercise), which is the counts-as reading working as intended. Kernel-level normative sorts would be type-over-claims drift, awaiting an example where the predicate vocabulary genuinely cannot express the distinction.
- **Proof-tree objects per claim.** Authorization logics suggest persisting a says/speaks-for proof object with every decision. The audit row plus trace already is the proof object - actor, gates evaluated, invariants checked, programme hash - in deterministic template-renderable form; formal proof-term machinery is unforced until delegation chains exist for it to prove something about (and richer actor lineage is already a named deferred item).
- **A business-vocabulary (SBVR-style) glossary layer.** A standardised term layer above the rules is a commercialisation concern, not a runtime one; the declared predicate vocabulary plus learner's-guide `.morph` comments carry the meaning today. Unforced.
